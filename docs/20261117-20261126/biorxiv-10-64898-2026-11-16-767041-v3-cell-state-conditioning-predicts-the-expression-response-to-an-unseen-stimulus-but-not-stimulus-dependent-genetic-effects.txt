## Abstract 16 

Background: Sequence-to-function models such as AlphaGenome 2 can be conditioned on a cell state supplied 17 

as a single-cell expression profile. Whether this conditioning captures the effects of an experimental stimulus, on 18 

gene expression or on the activity of regulatory variants, has not been tested. 19 

Results: We conditioned AlphaGenome 2 on interferon-β-stimulated and unstimulated CD14+ monocytes from 20 

a published single-cell dataset, using cells that were not used to measure the response. Predicted and measured 21

## Background 40 

Sequence-to-function models predict regulatory activity, such as chromatin accessibility and gene expression, di-41 

rectly from DNA sequence [1, 2]. Until recently, these models predicted a fixed set of cell types and assays, namely 42 

those represented in their training data. Two models released in late 2026 instead condition their predictions on a 43 

cell state supplied by the user as single-cell expression data, so that predictions can be requested for states outside 44 

the training catalogue [3, 4]. 45 

Many of the questions that motivate such predictions concern perturbed states: the response of a cell to a cytokine, 46 

a pathogen or a drug, and the regulatory variants whose effects appear only after stimulation. Studies of response 47 

eQTL in immune cells have shown that a substantial fraction of genetic effects on gene expression are modified by 48 

stimulation [5–8]. Reference catalogues, by contrast, are built largely from unperturbed tissue, so a user interested 49

Predicting transcriptional responses to perturbations from single-cell data is an established problem. Generative 51 

models such as scGen, CPA and GEARS predict responses for unseen cell types or perturbations [9–11], and scGen 52 

used the interferon-β-stimulated blood cells analysed here as a benchmark. Recent benchmarks show that simple 53 

linear baselines perform as well as deep learning models at predicting the effects of genetic perturbations [12]. 54 

Conditioned sequence models differ from these approaches in that they predict expression from DNA sequence 55 

given a state, and can therefore also be used to predict how sequence variants act in that state. Sequence models 56 

have so far explained differences in expression between individuals poorly [13, 14]. Independent evaluations of 57 

the first-generation AlphaGenome found that it registers in-silico promoter disruption locally but often fails to 58 

propagate it to downstream exons [15], and that annotations derived from it do not improve GWAS locus discovery 59 

beyond standard functional annotations [16]. None has evaluated cell-state conditioning. 60 

Two capabilities need to be distinguished. A conditioned model may reproduce how expression differs between 61 

two states without reproducing how the effects of individual variants differ between them, because the condition is 62 

a representation learned from expression profiles, which describe how a state looks on average rather than how it 63 

changes the consequences of individual variants. 64 

Here we test both capabilities in AlphaGenome 2 using interferon-β-stimulated CD14+ monocytes, a stimulated 65 

state that is not among the model’s built-in conditions. We ask whether the predicted expression response tracks 66 

the measured response, how the predicted response scales across fifteen conditioning states, and whether the same 67 

conditioning identifies stimulus-specific eQTL in two monocyte stimulation studies. We also describe what the 68 

built-in conditions of both model generations contain, and two analysis issues that produced a spurious result in 69 

our site-level analysis and are likely to affect similar evaluations. 70

## Discussion 194 

Conditioning AlphaGenome 2 on a single-cell profile of interferon-β-stimulated monocytes produced expression 195 

predictions that tracked the measured response, and the predicted response scaled with the similarity between each 196 

conditioning state and the target state. In contrast, we found no reliable evidence that the same conditioning identi-197 

fies stimulus-specific eQTL: the main analysis was inconclusive, and the only significant arm reflected a difference 198 

in resting-condition baselines. The model therefore reproduced a state-level expression response, whereas its ability 199 

to capture state-dependent variant effects remains unestablished. 200 

Expression-level prediction 201 

Our expression-level result is not directly comparable with benchmarks of perturbation-response models such as 202 

scGen [9], which predict the response of a held-out cell type from the responses observed in other cell types of 203 

the same dataset. Here the model received the stimulated state as input and predicted expression from sequence, 204 

although none of its built-in conditions is labelled with this stimulus. Whether a comparably simple baseline 205 

would perform as well, as reported for the prediction of genetic perturbation effects [12], remains to be tested. 206 

The calibration indicates that the predicted response was attenuated by about one quarter on average and that the 207 

attenuation was not uniform: stimulated T and NK states were consistently under-predicted, so users conditioning 208 

on such states should expect the response to be understated. Because the reliability of the measured axis was 0.983, 209

Site-level result and its possible explanations 211 

Neither catalogue contains an entry labelled with an experimental treatment. Across 4,430 first-generation tracks 212 

and 2,799 second-generation conditions, keyword searches over every text field returned no cytokine, Toll-like 213 

receptor ligand, virus, glucocorticoid or androgen treatment under either a narrow or a wide keyword list (Addi-214 

tional file 1, Table S3). The only matches were two GTEx skin labels (sun-exposed and not sun-exposed) and 215 

one GTEx label for Epstein-Barr-transformed lymphocytes, none of which is an experimental treatment under our 216 

definition (Methods). Lymphoblastoid lines appear under their cell-line names; GM12878 and the lines of its par-217 

ents (GM12891 and GM12892) alone contribute 131 tracks in the first-generation catalogue. The catalogues do 218 

carry cell states. Under the wide list (activated, activation, effector, memory), 29 first-generation tracks and 135 219 

second-generation conditions are labelled with a differentiation or activation state, such as memory and effector 220 

memory T cells, memory B cells, activated T cells and activated fibroblasts. Memory and effector labels occur in 221 

both catalogues, whereas the word activated occurs only in the second-generation conditions. 222 

The absence of a labelled treatment is one possible explanation for the site-level result, not a demonstrated cause. 223 

The same result is expected if sequence-to-expression models predict variation between genes better than variant 224 

effects between individuals [13, 14]; if the model underestimates the effects of causal eQTL variants, as reported 225 

for the first-generation AlphaGenome [21]; or if the conditions do not match, since none of the eQTL arms used 226 

interferon-β. The site-level analysis does not show that the conditioning carries no information about variant effects. 227 

It shows that we could not detect any in two studies and seven stimulus arms once both conditions were required 228 

to have a measurable predicted effect, with at most a few dozen sites per arm. A ground truth based on allelic 229 

activity measured directly in both conditions, such as reporter assays run under two treatments [22–24], would test 230 

the same question with an order of magnitude more sites. 231 

Implications for evaluating condition-dependent predictions 232 

Two issues in our site-level analysis are likely to affect other evaluations of condition-dependent predictions. First, 233 

labelling eQTL as condition-specific by credible-set membership retained mostly variants with significant effects 234 

in both conditions; condition specificity should instead be defined by testing the effect in both conditions or by 235 

modelling the conditions jointly [20]. Second, filtering a ratio of two predicted effects on the larger term leaves 236

Related work and future directions 247 

A literature search on 24 November 2026 found three independent evaluations of the first-generation model [15, 16, 248 

26], one fine-tuning of it [27], and no evaluation of cell-state conditioning. Our site-level result is consistent with 249 

the finding that annotations derived from these models do not improve common-variant discovery beyond standard 250 

functional annotations [16], although the two studies address different questions. An independently developed 251 

cell-state-conditioned model with its own variant-effect scoring [3] was not evaluated here; both analysis issues 252 

described above apply to it. Further work should test the expression-level result in other datasets, stimuli and cell 253 

types, use allelic activity measured in paired conditions as ground truth, build conditions from experimentally 254 

perturbed single-cell data, and compare conditioned models. 255 

Limitations 256 

The expression-level evidence comes from one cell type, one stimulus, one dataset and 119 genes, all on chromo-257 

somes 1 to 7 and X; the fifteen calibration conditions come from the same dataset and are correlated against the 258 

same measured response, so they are not independent replications. The conditioning and measurement cells share 259

the calibration was added after the analysis plan. The site-level analysis is underpowered in every arm: strict la-263 

belling leaves at most a few dozen visible sites per arm, some with only 6 or 7 per group and, under the gate on 264

## Conclusions 271 

Conditioning AlphaGenome 2 on a single-cell profile of a stimulated state reproduced the expression response of 272 

monocytes to interferon-β, and the predicted response scaled with the similarity of the conditioning state to the 273 

target. We found no reliable evidence that the same conditioning identifies stimulus-specific eQTL; the one signif-274 

icant result arose from an analysis artifact. Evaluations of conditioned models should assess state-level expression 275 

and state-dependent variant effects separately, define condition-specific eQTL by testing both conditions, require 276 

measurable effects in both conditions when comparing them, and report every planned comparison. 277 

## Methods 278 

Single-cell data 279 

All expression data come from GSE96583 batch 2 [17]: peripheral blood mononuclear cells from eight donors with 280 

systemic lupus erythematosus, assayed unstimulated and after six hours of interferon-β stimulation, with cell-type 281 

annotations and singlet calls from the series. Only singlets were used. Counts were pooled within each cell type 282 

and library and converted to counts per million (CPM); pooling, rather than treating cells as replicates, avoids the 283 

inflated false-discovery rates that follow from the latter [28]. 284 

Conditioning and scoring 285 

Each condition was built from 256 cells, the maximum accepted by the cell encoder, drawn once per library with 286 

default_rng(7) without replacement; all cells were used where a population had fewer than 256 (megakary-287 

ocytes, 122 and 134; dendritic cells, 203 and 228). Predictions were made with the denoised single-cell gene-288 

expression head under a 200 kb mean mask. 289 

The 40 input intervals were the 1,048,576-bp bins containing the most genes among the first 1,500 expressed genes 290

Measured overlap and calibration 312 

For each cell population, the measured overlap is the Spearman correlation between log2 of (that population’s CPM 313 

+ 1) over (control CD14+ monocyte CPM + 1) and the measured interferon-β response. The model value is the 314 

same correlation with the predicted log fold change in place of the measured one. Unstimulated populations use 315 

the control library and stimulated populations the stimulated library; the matched condition’s measured overlap is 316 

1 by construction. 317 

The calibration is an ordinary least-squares fit of model value on measured overlap over the fifteen points. The 318 

reliability of the measured axis was estimated by splitting each cell type’s cells in half, computing baseline and 319 

numerator each from its own half, and correlating the two resulting vectors across the fourteen non-matched condi-320 

tions; over 20 repetitions the half-sample reliability was 0.967 (SD 0.018), giving a Spearman–Brown full-sample 321 

reliability of 0.983. The disattenuated slope is the fitted slope divided by that value. 322 

Uncertainty and null distributions 323 

Confidence intervals are window-clustered bootstraps: windows were resampled with replacement, rows concate-324 

nated and the statistic recomputed (2,000 draws, seed 0). A gene-level bootstrap would understate the uncertainty 325 

because genes within a window are correlated; both approaches understate it relative to resampling the conditioning 326 

cells. The split-half null halves the control cells, builds a condition from each half and scores the resulting log ratio 327 

against the measured response (50 draws). In one draw, predictions were available for only 62 of the 119 analysis 328 

genes, because scoring failed for part of one half’s intervals and only genes scored in both halves enter a draw; that 329 

draw is included in the reported distribution, and excluding it leaves none of the remaining 49 draws at or above the 330 

observed value. The depth comparison binomially thins the disjoint counts to the depth of the earlier shared-cell 331 

measurement (20 draws) and compares the resulting correlation with the shared-cell value at that depth. 332

Site-level analysis 333 

Fine-mapped eQTL were taken from eQTL Catalogue release 8 [18] for two monocyte studies [5, 6], fine-mapped 334 

with SuSiE [29]. A site is activation-specific if it enters only the stimulated condition’s credible set, resting-specific 335 

if it enters only the resting one, and shared if it enters both. Under the strict definition used throughout, a site 336 

additionally requires an absolute z score below 2 in the other condition’s full summary statistics; sites not tested 337 

there are counted separately (variant, gene or probe not tested) and are not treated as absence of effect, and shared 338 

sites require the same effect direction in both conditions. 339 

The z scores for the other condition were retrieved from the remote tabix-indexed summary statistics. For the same 340 

variant, beta is identical in the credible-set file and in the nominal summary statistics of a study, but the standard 341 

error in the credible-set files is systematically smaller (median 1.55% in Fairfax and 3.24% in Quach), so the two 342 

files do not carry interchangeable z scores. The validation criterion written before the lookup, agreement within 343 

0.01, therefore failed for 1,482 of 1,506 Fairfax and 3,617 of 3,627 Quach comparisons. After the labels had 344 

been built and the first group comparison made, we replaced it with sign agreement of 100%, a rank correlation 345 

above 0.999 and at least 99.5% agreement on the absolute-z-below-2 classification; these thresholds were set after 346 

the validation results were known. Under them, Fairfax agreed on 100% of 1,506 comparisons (rank correlation 347 

0.99996) and Quach on 99.945% of 3,627, with the two disagreements at absolute z of 1.898 and 2.020. Twenty 348 

sites not found in the summary statistics were rechecked with a wider window, and no missed records were found. 349 

The revision changed how the lookup was validated, not which sites the labels contain. 350 

The test statistic is log2 of the ratio of absolute predicted effects, stimulated condition over resting condition. Fol-351 

lowing the plan, arm-level comparisons use a visibility gate on the larger of the two absolute effects (> 0.1); because 352 

that gate leaves the smaller term unconstrained, results under a gate on the smaller term are reported for the influenza 353 

arm in Results and for all seven arms in Additional file 1, Table S4. Effect sizes are reported as the bounded index, 354 

the difference of the two terms over their sum. All medians of site-level quantities are per-site medians: for each 355 

site the log ratio and the bounded index are computed, and the median of those values is reported. Group compar-356 

isons used two-sided Mann–Whitney tests. Robustness was assessed by leave-one-out, placebo splits of the control 357 

condition, removal of sites shared between the two studies, and a stricter label threshold. 358
