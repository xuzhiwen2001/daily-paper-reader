Abstract 
16 
Single-cell foundation models are general tools for resolving cell identities and disease states, 
17 
but struggle to interpret changes in expression magnitude in biological language, to transfer 
18 
to sparse spatial measurements, and to explain cellular niches. We introduce LingoCell, a 
19 
foundation model connecting single-cell and spatial transcriptomics. It aligns cell 
20 
representations with language while keeping gene expression editable, supporting cell 
21 
annotation, cross-batch integration, description generation, and disease-language readouts of 
22 
single-gene and combinatorial in silico perturbations. Extended to tissues, it annotates cells 
23 
without single-cell references, learns identity-conditioned niche embeddings, and performs 
24 
.

Introduction 
31 
Single-cell foundation models learn gene coordination by pretraining on tens of millions of 
32 
cells to support cell annotation, batch integration, and perturbation analysis, yet their 
33 
perturbation and interpretation capabilities remain constrained by how expression is 
34 
represented. Geneformer represents cells as gene sequences ranked by relative expression and 
35 
identified cardiomyopathy candidate targets through in silico deletion,1 but rank-based 
36 
encoding records only relative gene order, preventing magnitude adjustments that preserve 
37 
rank order from entering the model. scGPT jointly models gene identity and discretized 
38 
expression values to support annotation, integration, and fine-tuned perturbation prediction,2 
39 
while CellFM scaled pretraining to roughly 100 million human cells.3 Although these models 
40 
detect magnitude changes, their perturbation outputs are embedding shifts or predicted 
41 
expression profiles rather than semantic readouts defined by disease descriptions. More 
42 
fundamentally, none is aligned during pretraining with natural language describing cell 
43 
identity, function, and disease, making existing biological concepts difficult to use for 
44 
identifying cells in new data or interpreting molecular changes. 
45 
Aligning cell representations with language brings these concepts directly into the model. 
46 
LangCell achieves zero-shot annotation through cell–text contrastive learning but retains 
47 
rank-based encoding and does not generate cell descriptions.4 CellWhisperer links 
48 
transcriptome–text alignment to a large language model for free-text cell retrieval and 
49 
.

53 
Even when a model interprets and edits a cell's own expression through language, models 
54

55 
Platforms such as Visium HD have advanced spatial transcriptomics to near-single-cell 
56

57

58

59

60

61

62 
single-cell identity knowledge, leaving open how to sustain cell annotation and semantic 
63 
understanding in spatial data. 
64 
Maintaining stable cell identities in tissues is also fundamental to understanding niches: the 
65 
same environment conveys different information to cells with different functions, and 
66 
neighbor features relevant to a T cell need not be those to which a tumor cell responds. 
67 
Existing methods mainly define niches from the environment itself: BANKSY combines a 
68 
cell's own expression, neighborhood mean expression, and spatial gradients,11 whereas 
69 
DECIPHER uses cross-scale contrastive learning to represent molecular identity and spatial 
70 
environment separately.12 Neither environmental representation is queried by the central cell's 
71 
identity, making environmental differences difficult to interpret in a specific cellular context. 
72

We therefore introduce LingoCell, a foundation model connecting cell identity, disease 
76 
concepts, and tissue environment through language-aligned expression representations. 
77 
Pretrained on more than 11 million single cells and their paired descriptions, LingoCell 
78 
jointly encodes gene identity and expression level and aligns them with text, supporting 
79 
clustering, cross-batch integration, zero-shot annotation, and description generation without 
80 
fine-tuning; because expression magnitude remains editable after alignment, it can also adjust 
81 
one or more genes and read cellular responses using interchangeable disease descriptions. 
82 
Transferred to tissues through measurement adaptation, LingoCell annotates Visium HD data 
83 
at single-cell resolution without a single-cell reference, uses the central cell's identity as a 
84 
query to build observer-dependent niche embeddings integrating continuous neighbor 
85 
molecular states and spatial structure, and performs spatial in silico perturbation by replacing 
86 
and editing neighbor states. Using LingoCell, we found that co-downregulating alveolar 
87 
differentiation programs and co-upregulating malignant programs synergistically increased 
88 
cellular alignment with disease descriptions in lung adenocarcinoma, distinguished fibroblast 
89 
environments with similar neighbor compositions but different molecular states in normal 
90 
colon, and revealed distinct program priorities for T cells, macrophages, and tumor cells 
91 
reading the same fibroblast state in colorectal cancer. As a unified foundation model spanning 
92 
cell atlases and tissues in situ, LingoCell enables researchers to investigate the meaning of 
93 
genes or neighbor programs across single-cell and tissue scales, advancing analysis from 
94 
describing what cells are to interpreting what molecular changes mean.

Results 
96 
Overview of LingoCell 
97 
LingoCell connects single-cell and spatial transcriptomics within a unified architecture (Fig. 
98 
1): the single-cell stage aligns editable gene expression with natural language to support 
99 
representation analysis, description generation, and disease-semantic perturbation (Fig. 1a–d), 
100 
whereas the spatial stage transfers to tissues in situ through measurement adaptation to 
101 
resolve identity-conditioned local environments and perform neighborhood in silico 
102 
perturbation (Fig. 1e–g). 
103 
Single-cell pretraining uses more than 11 million single cells and paired descriptions from 
104 
CELLxGENE (Fig. 1a).13 The expression encoder reads gene identity and expression level, 
105 
while the text encoder reads cell-type, tissue, and state descriptions; cross-modal contrastive 
106 
learning and cell-type prototype learning bring both into a shared representation space, and 
107 
expression recovery preserves gene-level information. Frozen identity embeddings directly 
108 
support annotation, clustering, and cross-batch integration (Fig. 1b), while gene 
109 
representations pass through an adapter to a LLaMA decoder to generate natural language 
110 
describing cell identity, tissue origin, and function (Fig. 1c). Because expression magnitude 
111 
remains editable, adjusting one or more genes in a fixed cellular context and re-encoding 
112 
yields a semantic shift (ΔF) along the direction defined by normal and disease descriptions as 
113 
a disease-semantic readout; switching the disease description without changing the 
114 
perturbation interprets the same expression change against different diseases (Fig. 1d). 
115 
On entering the spatial stage, because platforms such as Visium HD capture expression more 
116 
sparsely at single-cell resolution and differ systematically from single-cell sequencing, 
117 
LingoCell first performs measurement adaptation training on single-cell data, simulating 
118 
spatial capture depth and sparsity while constraining the same cell to retain consistent identity 
119 
embeddings across observation conditions (Fig. 1e). The adapted encoder then joins a spatial 
120 
.

126 
Niche embeddings extend in silico perturbation to tissue neighborhoods (Fig. 1g): fixing the 
127 
central cell (observer) and its spatial context, real states of a given neighbor type from across 
128 
the section (donors) are placed into its neighborhood one at a time, and the shift of the 
129

130

131

132 
tests effect direction and compares how different observers weight the same neighbor state.

LingoCell translates single-cell expression profiles into molecularly grounded natural 
179 
language descriptions 
180 
Beyond querying cells with given text, LingoCell can also generate open-ended descriptions 
181 
de novo from transcriptomes: gene representations from the frozen expression encoder pass 
182 
through an adapter to the language decoder, translating expression directly into coherent 
183 
sentences summarizing cell identity, tissue origin, and function (Fig. 1a,c). Compared with 
184 
Cell2Text, which generates descriptions from single-cell expression,6 LingoCell achieved 
185 
RoBERTa- and BioBERT-based BERTScores of 96.60% and 94.20% (Cell2Text: 96.28% 
186 
and 93.90%) and extracted cell-type, tissue, and disease F1 scores of 78.72%, 80.65%, and 
187 
79.50%, all exceeding Cell2Text (Fig. 3a), with consistent gains in field accuracy and lexical 
188 
metrics including BLEU and ROUGE (Supplementary Fig. S4a,b and Supplementary Note 
189 
2). Generated text also links identity to biological function: kidney cells expressing AQP3, 
190 
FXYD4, and SCNN1B were described as renal principal cells regulating sodium and 
191 
potassium balance, and brain cells expressing PLP1, CNP, and MBP as oligodendrocytes 
192 
forming axonal myelin in the central nervous system (Fig. 3b; additional examples in 
193 
Supplementary Fig. S4c).

LingoCell resolves disease-associated cell states and gene programs through semantic 
208 
queries and in silico perturbation 
209 
The unified expression–language space enables free text to serve directly as a semantic probe 
210 
of cellular function and disease states. In the lung single-nucleus atlas of Melms et al. 
211 
(GSE171524), comprising COVID-19 decedents and controls,25 keeping identity embeddings 
212 
fixed while querying “Angiogenesis” concentrated high-response cells in endothelial cells, 
213 
switching to “Epithelial–mesenchymal transition” (EMT) shifted the response to 
214 
mesenchymal fibroblasts, and “Complement system” highlighted immune populations 
215 
including B cells, antigen-presenting cells, and T cells (Fig. 4a). These functional concepts 
216 
map biological questions directly onto cell populations without prior conversion into marker 
217 
gene lists.

Switching queries to disease descriptions distinguishes state changes across cell populations 
219 
within an organ. Using CELLxGENE lung disease data, we compared normal-to-disease 
220 
representation changes against language directions for inflammation-associated versus 
221 
fibrotic/obstructive lung diseases (Fig. 4b): alveolar and elicited macrophages were biased 
222 
toward the inflammatory side, conventional dendritic cells and classical monocytes 
223 
responded strongly in both directions, and alveolar type II (AT2) cells were biased toward the 
224 
fibrotic/obstructive side. Comparing COVID-19 and lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) 
225 
directions, the former highlighted classical monocytes, dendritic cells, and subsets of 
226 
lymphocytes, whereas the latter involved immune, vascular endothelial, and alveolar 
227 
epithelial cells, with capillary endothelial cells showing opposite alignment across the two 
228 
directions (Fig. 4c); AT2 cell changes aligned with the LUAD description, echoing the AT2-
229 
related transcriptional lineage in human LUAD and the attenuation of alveolar identity during 
230 
tumor progression.26 
231 
Interchangeable disease directions also screen for disease-associated candidate genes in 
232 
healthy cells. In healthy alveolar macrophages primarily from the Human Lung Cell Atlas 
233 
(HLCA),27 we upregulated single genes in silico and read responses against LUAD, 
234 
pulmonary sarcoidosis, and COVID-19 descriptions (Fig. 4d): CD163 and LIPA were 
235

236

237

238

239

240

241

242

244

245

246

247

251

253

258

Discussion 
406 
LingoCell unifies editable molecular inputs with interpretable biological references within a 
407 
single foundation model: the single-cell stage supports precise editing through joint encoding 
408 
of gene identity and expression magnitude while providing disease-concept readouts through 
409 
natural-language alignment, and the spatial stage uses a cell's own identity as the query 
410 
reference for reading neighborhood states. Single-cell and in situ tissue analyses thus follow 
411 
the same paradigm, modifying molecular inputs within a defined cellular context and 
412 
interpreting responses along biologically intelligible directions. 
413 
Semantic perturbation requires that expression changes both enter the model and acquire 
414 
biological meaning: expression models such as scGPT are magnitude-sensitive but lack 
415 
language references,2 whereas alignment models such as LangCell have language references 
416 
but are restricted by rank-based encoding to deleting or fronting genes.1,4 LingoCell unifies 
417 
both in a single encoder so that genes can be adjusted stepwise across ordered levels and read 
418 
.
