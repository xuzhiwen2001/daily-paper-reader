Abstract 15 

Single-cell perturbation atlases now measure cellular responses across millions of cells, but the number 16 

of possible combinations of biological contexts, perturbations, doses and other experimental variables 17 

make exhaustive measurements infeasible. Here, we present GENESIS (Generative ENErgy-based 18 

Single-cell Intervention Simulator), a generative framework that predicts single-cell response distribu-19 

tions directly from the covariates defining an experimental condition. GENESIS uses a lightweight 20 

neural network trained with the energy score, a strictly proper scoring rule for distributions. We 21 

introduce an experiment-calibrated evaluation framework in which prediction error is interpreted rela-22 

tive to the reproducibility of repeated measurements of the same condition. Across the Tahoe-100M 23 

small-molecule atlas and a large PBMC cytokine atlas, GENESIS significantly outperforms existing gen-24 

erative models and statistical baselines and approaches experimental reproducibility on independently 25 

repeated conditions. Importantly, GENESIS remains accurate when trained on only a fraction of the 26 

available experimental conditions or cells, and generated profiles recover experimentally observed 27 

context-dependent transcriptional programs. At comparable training-cell budgets, GENESIS achieves 28 

more accurate predictions when measurements span more experimental conditions rather than pro-29 

filing fewer conditions more deeply. Together, these findings suggest a complementary strategy for 30 

perturbation studies: broader, lower-depth measurements can expand experimental coverage, while 31 

GENESIS augments sparsely sampled populations and predicts responses in unmeasured conditions. 32

## Introduction 34 

Cells remodel their transcriptomes in response to genetic, chemical and environmental perturbations. 35 

Single-cell perturbation atlases now measure these responses at unprecedented scale: Tahoe-100M 36 

profiles more than 100 million cells across approximately 55,000 drug–cell-line–dose conditions 1,37

million cells across diverse cytokine perturbations 2. Yet even datasets of this scale sample only a 39 

small fraction of the possible experimental space, which spans factors such as biological context 40 

(such as cell lines or donors), perturbation, dose, timing and other experimental variables. This 41 

vast combinatorial space makes exhaustive measurement infeasible. Computational models could 42 

instead use measured conditions to predict outcomes across the much larger space of unobserved 43 

experimental settings, but this strategy requires both data-efficient learning and a meaningful 44 

standard for judging generated predictions. 45 

Several generative approaches have been developed to predict single-cell perturbation responses. 46 

Most formulate the problem as learning a mapping from a measured population of control cells to a 47 

perturbed population, using set-based transformers 3, variational autoencoders 4, optimal transport 5,48 

flow matching 6 or diffusion models 7. Yet this mapping is not directly observed experimentally: 49 

because single-cell sequencing is destructive, control and perturbed populations consist of differ-50 

ent cells rather than paired measurements before and after treatment. Evaluating these models 51 

presents a further difficulty 8–10 : although new methods are routinely benchmarked against held-out 52 

perturbation responses, it remains unclear how close we are to predicting responses as reliably 53 

as they can be measured. Even when the same cell line is exposed to the same drug and dose 54 

on separate experimental plates, the measured responses can differ owing to finite sampling and 55 

variation between experimental runs. Without a reference for this experimental reproducibility, 56 

improvements over existing methods do not establish how close predictions come to the agreement 57 

achievable between independent experimental repeats. 58 

Here, we introduce GENESIS (Generative ENErgy-based Single-cell Intervention Simulator), 59 

which models cellular response distributions directly from the covariates defining an experimental 60 

condition. In the datasets studied here, these specify the cell line or donor and cell type, the drug 61 

or cytokine applied, its dose where applicable, and the experimental batch. A lightweight neural 62 

network uses these covariates to generate individual cell profiles without requiring measured 63 

control cells at inference. The model is trained using the energy score, a strictly proper scoring rule 64 

that enables learning distributions directly from individual observations, without requiring paired 65 

cells or fixed-size population targets. The framework accommodates different sets of experimental 66 

covariates and incomplete coverage of their possible combinations. 67 

We evaluate GENESIS on Tahoe-100M and the PBMC cytokine atlas, demonstrating improved 68 

prediction of held-out experimental conditions relative to existing generative models and statistical 69 

baselines. To contextualize performance, we use independent experimental replicates in Tahoe-70 

100M to establish a reproducibility benchmark, which GENESIS approaches. We further show that 71 

GENESIS maintains strong performance with substantially reduced training data and that generated 72 

transcriptomes capture biologically meaningful, context-dependent responses. Together, these 73 

findings establish a framework for predicting cellular responses across subsampled experimental 74 

spaces and inform potential strategies for designing more efficient perturbation studies. 75 

## GENESIS generates perturbation responses from experimental 76 

## covariates 77 

We model a single cell’s transcriptome as a draw from a distribution conditioned on the covari-78 

ates specifying its experimental condition. For ease of reference, we refer to these covariates in 79 

three groups: biological context (e.g., cell line), perturbation (e.g., drug, dose), and experimen-80 

tal replication context (e.g., plate identifier), which collectively define the condition. Denoting a 81 

condition as c and the cells’ expression as Y , GENESIS learns the conditional distribution P(Y | c).82 

Each covariate is represented by a learned embedding, which maps the value of the covariate to a 83 

numerical representation. The embeddings defining a condition are concatenated with random 84

This formulation is particularly natural for single-cell perturbation experiments. Because single-87 

cell sequencing is destructive, control and perturbed cells are unpaired samples from condition-level 88 

distributions: the pre-perturbation state of an individual assayed cell is never observed. GENESIS 89 

therefore requires neither measured control cells at inference nor an assumed correspondence 90 

between individual control and perturbed cells. Instead, a control measurement (i.e., the null 91 

perturbation) is simply represented using a dedicated value for the perturbation covariate. 92 

GENESIS is trained to recover the full conditional distribution using the energy score 11 as 93 

a loss function (Methods). The energy score can be computed from a single observed cell of a 94 

condition. It balances two requirements: generated transcriptomes should lie close to the observed 95 

transcriptome but also differ from one another, which encourages the model to capture variation 96 

across sampled cells during training (Fig. 1b). More fundamentally, the energy score is a strictly 97 

proper scoring rule: averaged over observations, the true distribution is its unique optimum. This 98 

framework thus enables learning heterogeneous condition-level responses directly from individual 99 

cells, without paired observations or fixed-size population targets. We additionally include a 100 

variogram term 12 in the loss function that encourages the model to reproduce the average magnitude 101 

of expression differences between pairs of genes within cells, helping capture gene–gene dependence 102 

and improving convergence in practice (Methods). 103 

## Experimental reproducibility provides a reference for model 104 

## performance 105 

Various metrics have been proposed to measure agreement between predicted and measured tran-106 

scriptomes, including direction-matched differential-expression gene overlap 3, control-relative 107 

Pearson correlation 13,14 and energy distance 15 . While these metrics each measure useful aspects of 108 

concordance, they are difficult to interpret without a reference for what level of agreement is experi-109 

mentally achievable. We therefore anchor predictive performance to experimental reproducibility :110 

how closely an independently repeated experiment reproduces the original measurement (Fig. 1c, 111 

Methods). When the same condition is assayed in different experimental batches, such as on dif-112 

ferent plates in Tahoe-100M, disagreement between the two measurements captures both finite 113 

sampling and variation introduced by repeating the experiment 16,17 . We refer to a prediction as 114 

reaching experimental reproducibility when its agreement with the measured response is compara-115 

ble to that between independent experimental repeats. Because independent repeats are available 116 

for only a subset of conditions, we additionally introduce split-half reproducibility for conditions 117 

measured only once. Cells from a single experiment are randomly divided into two equally sized 118 

populations and compared, with the partition redrawn 20 times and results averaged across draws 119 

(Fig. 1c, Methods). Split-half reproducibility captures finite-sampling variability without the addi-120 

tional variation introduced by repeating the experiment, hence it provides an overly-optimistic but 121 

still informative reference for scenarios lacking experimental reproducibility data. 122 

Fig. 1d compares the experimental and split-half reproducibility benchmarks in Tahoe-100M 123 

as a function of the number of cells used in the calculation. Averaged over the one hundred most 124 

deeply profiled Tahoe-100M conditions measured on two plates, both benchmarks show relatively 125 

high experimental variability, but split-half reproducibility improves more sharply with cell count. 126 

Additional cells therefore reduce finite-sampling variability but do not eliminate variation introduced 127 

by repeating the experiment. Differential-expression analysis, a routine readout of perturbation 128 

experiments, is strongly affected: even at 1,600 cells per arm, two plates share on average only 129 

about 20 of their top 100 differentially expressed genes, compared with more than 60 for two 130

## GENESIS performance approaches experimental reproducibility 133 

We first evaluated GENESIS on its ability to predict perturbation responses in held-out biological 134 

contexts (i.e., cell lines for Tahoe-100M and donors for PBMC), following the train /test splits and 135 

selected gene panels introduced in prior work 3,7 . For each cell line or donor selected for evaluation, 136 

controls and a minority of randomly selected perturbations are observed during training, while 137 

the majority of perturbations are reserved for testing. The model must therefore infer unobserved 138 

responses using perturbation measurements from other biological contexts and the limited training 139 

measurements available for the same cell line or donor (Methods, Extended Data Fig. 1a,c). We 140 

benchmarked GENESIS against PerturbDiff 7, STATE 3 and CellFlow 6, together with linear, mean-141 

based and nearest-neighbor baselines (Methods, Supplementary Methods). We scored predictions 142 

with three metrics that capture complementary aspects of a response: direction-matched top-100 143

We first assessed how closely GENESIS performance approaches the reproducibility of the 150 

experiments themselves. Averaged across independently repeated Tahoe-100M conditions, GEN-151 

ESIS performance reached experimental reproducibility in Pearson ∆ (GENESIS: 0.430 [95% CI 152 

0.381-0.482 ]; experimental reproducibility: 0.432 [0.378-0.491 ]; p = 0.56) and energy distance 153 

(GENESIS: 0.158 [0.147-0.170 ]; experimental: 0.154 [0.143-0.166 ]; p = 0.36), and fell only 3.4% 154 

short in the top-100 DEG overlap (GENESIS: 0.289 [0.263-0.317 ]; experimental: 0.299 [0.271-155 

0.330 ]; p = 8 × 10 −4) (Fig. 2a, Methods, Supplementary Table 1). All other models and baselines 156 

differed significantly from experimental reproducibility across all three metrics. Experimental repro-157 

ducibility itself varied substantially across conditions. When plate pairs were ranked by agreement 158 

between experimental repeats, GENESIS tracked this variation most closely (Fig. 2b). 159 

Experimental replicates cover only a subset of the held-out conditions in Tahoe-100M ( N = 530) 160 

and are absent from the PBMC cytokine atlas, where each donor was assayed on its own plate, 161 

so plate and donor cannot be separated (Extended Data Fig. 3). We therefore next evaluated all 162 

held-out conditions ( N = 4211 on Tahoe-100M, N = 4268 on PBMC cytokine atlas), using split-half 163 

reproducibility to contextualize performance. GENESIS showed state-of-the-art performance on 164 

both datasets (Fig. 2c). In Tahoe-100M, direction-matched top-100 DEG overlap increased from an 165 

average of 30.7 genes [95% CI 29.7-31.6 ] for the next-best method (STATE) to 51.3 [50.5-52.1 ] for 166 

GENESIS, a 67% increase; in the PBMC cytokine atlas, from 37.3 [34.5-40.2 ] to 41.7 [38.8-44.6 ], a 167 

12% increase. The split-half reproducibility values are 66.8 and 67.1 for Tahoe-100M and PBMC, 168 

respectively. GENESIS also achieved the highest Pearson ∆ and the lowest energy distance on both 169 

datasets (Fig. 2c). 170 

We further evaluated a complementary perturbation-generalization setting. Whereas the previ-171 

ous setting withholds most perturbation responses from selected cell lines or donors for testing, here 172 

we withhold most cell lines or donors from selected perturbations for testing. Specifically, in Tahoe-173 

100M, test perturbations were observed in only 1, 5, 15 or 20 training cell lines, while in the PBMC 174 

atlas, test perturbations were observed in only 1, 3 or 5 training donors (Methods; Extended Data 175 

Fig. 1b,d). Across 4,023 held-out conditions in Tahoe-100M and 3,456 in the PBMC cytokine atlas, 176 

GENESIS again outperformed all competing methods on all three metrics (Extended Data Fig. 4). 177 

## GENESIS maintains strong performance with reduced training data 178 

We next investigated how predictive performance depends on the amount of training data. To test 179 

biological-context generalization, we retrained GENESIS on reduced versions of the Tahoe-100M 180 

dataset. Here, we used either fewer experimental conditions or fewer cells per condition, while 181 

matching the control and perturbed-cell budgets and keeping the original test set fixed (Methods). 182 

Remarkably, using only ∼5% of the available perturbed training cells (4.6 million of 89.5 million), 183 

GENESIS outperformed all competing generative models and statistical baselines trained on the full 184 

dataset across all three metrics (Fig. 3a, Supplementary Table 2). Relative to the strongest full-data 185 

competitors, GENESIS increased top-100 DEG overlap by approximately 50% and Pearson ∆ by 186 

30% (STATE), while reducing energy distance by approximately 60% (context-mean baseline). Thus, 187 

GENESIS achieved state-of-the-art performance with 20-fold fewer perturbed-cell measurements. 188

## programs 208 

The value of generated single-cell profiles ultimately depends on whether they support the same 209 

biological interpretations as measured cells. We therefore asked whether GENESIS could reproduce 210 

transcriptional responses at the level of signaling pathways, a common readout of perturbation 211 

experiments. We used PROGENy 18 , which infers pathway activity from experimentally derived 212 

transcriptional footprints, providing a measure of downstream signaling responses rather than the 213 

expression of pathway components themselves. We evaluated all 14 pathways across 55 compounds 214 

in the five held-out Tahoe-100M cell lines, quantifying responses as PROGENy-weighted mean 215 

log 2 fold changes relative to matched controls. For this analysis, we expanded the utilized gene 216 

panel to encompass those used in PROGENy-defined pathways and trained GENESIS on only 217 

5% of the available training cells (“fewer cells” configuration in Fig. 3a; Methods). Generated 218 

profiles closely reproduced the measured patterns of pathway induction and suppression (Fig. 4). 219 

Quantitatively, across conditions measured on independent plates, experimental repeats showed 220 

an average Pearson correlation of 0.455 [95% CI 0.415-0.499 ] across the 14 pathway responses. 221 

GENESIS achieved a comparable average correlation of 0.402 [0.370-0.439 ] with the measured 222 

responses, outperforming all other models and statistical baselines (Extended Data Fig. 7). 223 

We next asked whether GENESIS could distinguish responses shared across biological contexts 224 

from those specific to individual cell lines. This distinction is particularly important in cancer, where 225 

the same treatment can produce different downstream effects depending on genetic background. 226 

We examined two KRAS-mutant cell lines, HOP62 lung cancer cells and Hs 766T pancreatic cancer 227 

cells, which exhibited both shared and distinct responses (Fig. 4). The MEK inhibitor trametinib and 228 

the RAS(ON) inhibitor RMC-6236 (daraxonrasib) 19 both suppressed EGFR- and MAPK-associated 229 

transcriptional activity in the two lines, and GENESIS reproduced these shared responses (Fig. 4). 230 

Yet their inflammatory responses diverged: both treatments increased JAK-STAT and TNF α pathway 231 

scores in Hs 766T, while suppressing them in HOP62. GENESIS captured these opposing responses, 232 

despite not observing these compound-cell-line combinations during training (Fig. 4). 233 

## Discussion 234 

GENESIS combines a conditional generator with a distributional training objective to predict 235 

single-cell perturbation responses for unseen combinations of experimental covariates. Across 236 

both Tahoe-100M and the PBMC cytokine atlas, it outperforms existing generative and statistical 237 

approaches and, when independent repeats are available, achieves performance comparable to 238 

experimental reproducibility. Because GENESIS conditions directly on recorded experimental 239 

covariates, it can also be readily adapted to different experimental designs. 240 

Beyond predictive performance, our pathway analysis suggests that GENESIS recovers context-241 

dependent transcriptional programs. The captured differences between HOP62 and Hs 766T cell 242 

lines may be particularly relevant to cancer therapy, where compensatory signaling can influence 243 

responses to targeted inhibitors. The suppression of TNF α-associated transcription in HOP62 is 244 

consistent with the established oncogenic role of KRAS in promoting inflammatory signaling 20–22 .245 

Conversely, increased JAK-STAT activity in Hs 766T is consistent with reports of compensatory 246 

STAT3 activation following MAPK inhibition, a potential resistance mechanism that has motivated 247 

combined MEK and JAK /STAT3 inhibition in pancreatic cancer 23,24 and is now under further clinical 248 

investigation 25 . The response to RMC-6236 also parallels interferon-associated signaling reported 249 

in pancreatic tumors following prolonged RAS inhibition, which has been linked to adaptive cell-250 

state changes and therapeutic resistance 26 . By recovering these contrasting responses alongside 251

GENESIS 391 

Conditional generator. 392 

For each experimental condition c, GENESIS generates individual cell profiles by combining learned 393 

representations of the experimental covariates with random noise. The covariates specify the 394 

experimental condition, while independent noise draws allow the model to generate different cells 395 

from the corresponding distribution. For Tahoe-100M, the covariates consisted of a cell line (e.g., 396 

C32), a compound (e.g., amsacrine), a dose and a plate. For the PBMC cytokine atlas, the covariates 397 

consisted of a donor, a cell-type, a cytokine and a well. 398 

Each covariate value is mapped to a learned d-dimensional embedding, with d = 256 and 399 

coordinates initialized independently from N (0, 1 /d). Thus, the initialization standard deviation 400 

is 1 /pd = 1/16. These embeddings are concatenated with a d-dimensional noise vector whose 401 

coordinates are drawn independently from U [−p3/d, p3/d]. Since a uniform distribution on 402 

[−a, a] has variance a2/3, these bounds give the noise coordinates variance 1 /d, matching the 403 

embedding coordinates at initialization. Consequently, each input block has unit expected squared 404 

norm, so the embeddings and noise enter the network on the same scale at initialization. 405 

A multilayer perceptron with hidden widths 4,096 and 8,192 and GELU activations generates 406 

an expression profile, with a rectified linear output giving non-negative log (1 + count ) values. No 407 

observed cells are required as inputs. Controls are represented by a dedicated perturbation value, 408 

and independent noise draws generate cells from the same condition. 409

Statistical baselines. 476 

We compared GENESIS with five baselines fitted only on training conditions, weighting each 477 

condition equally. The context mean predicts the average expression across training perturbations 478 

in the same cell line and plate. The perturbation mean adds the average response to the target 479 

perturbation across training contexts to the target context’s control mean. The additive model 480 

combines the control mean with an overall mean response and separate context and perturbation 481 

effects, without an interaction term. Linear ridge regression 14 estimates the perturbation response in 482 

a 100-dimensional gene space derived from training pseudobulks, with regularization strength 0.1. 483 

Nearest-neighbor selects the training context most similar to the target context across their shared 484 

training perturbations and copies its measured cells under the target perturbation on the same 485 

plate. Similarity was measured by cosine distance between responses projected onto 100 principal 486 

components. Full equations and fitting procedures for all baselines are provided in Supplementary 487

Data efficiency and scaling 559 

Fewer conditions or fewer cells. 560 

To ask whether a fixed experimental budget is better spent measuring more conditions or more 561 

cells per condition, we reduced the Tahoe-100M biological-context training set in two ways while 562 

keeping the test set unchanged. In the fewer-conditions setting, we retained all cells from either 5% 563 

or 25% of the 52,885 perturbed training conditions, defined by cell line, compound and dose with 564 

plates pooled. Conditions were selected using hierarchical balanced stratified sampling (see next 565 

paragraph). In the fewer-cells setting, we retained the same number of perturbed cells, sampled 566 

uniformly across all training conditions. All control cells were retained in both settings. The two 567 

training sets therefore contained the same numbers of control and perturbed cells and differed only 568 

in how broadly the perturbed measurements were distributed across conditions. Reduced datasets 569 

were trained for 5 / f epochs, where f is the retained fraction of conditions, ensuring at least as 570 

many optimizer steps as for the full training set. 571 

Hierarchical balanced stratified sampling. 572 

When subsampling conditions, we wanted to reduce the size of the training set without preferentially 573 

retaining the most densely measured parts of the dataset. We therefore selected conditions so 574 

that coverage remained approximately balanced across cell lines, compounds and doses, while 575 

also encouraging each compound to be represented across diverse tissue types. The sampler first 576 

assigns inclusion weights to all measured cell line, compound and dose combinations so that their 577 

expected representation is balanced along each of these three axes. For each compound and dose, 578 

these weights determine how many cell lines are retained. Cell lines are then sampled without 579 

replacement, with lower probability given to tissues already represented for that compound. This 580 

procedure is repeated until the target fraction of conditions is reached. The full algorithm is given 581 

in Supplementary Methods. 582

Nearest neighbor. 858 

We introduce a nearest-neighbor baseline that predicts a perturbation’s effect by copying its observed 859
