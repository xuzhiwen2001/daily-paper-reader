# Where single-cell foundation models help: a benchmark of annotation, perturbation and regulatory inference against simple baselines 

Francesca Cupaioli and Ivan Merelli* Institute for Biomedical Technologies, National Research Council (CNR-ITB), Via Fratelli Cervi 93, 20054 Segrate (MI), Italy *Corresponding author: Ivan Merelli, ivan.merelli@itb.cnr.it 

## Abstract 

Single-cell foundation models (FMs) are proposed as general tools for cell-type annotation, in silico perturbation and gene-regulatory-network (GRN) inference. To identify where they add value, we benchmarked five FMs (scGPT, Geneformer V1 and V2, Nicheformer, scFoundation), CellOracle, supervised perturbation models including STATE, and task-matched simple baselines on the same data, from K562 Perturb-seq screens and primary cells to healthy-donor and isogenic leukodystrophy brain organoids, a public organoid CRISPR screen and lung cancer biopsies, using each model with its recommended settings and with tuned fine-tuning. FMs were most useful when labels had to be transferred across species: from human to mouse lung, Nicheformer reached 0.97 accuracy, above every baseline including CellTypist with tuned regularisation (0.94), and fine-tuned Geneformer V2 transferred labels from mouse to human with macro-F1 0.88, against at most 0.74 for baselines trained on the same cells. Fine-tuned Geneformer V2 annotated organoid cells more accurately than principal components (0.74 versus 0.71). In K562, zero-shot perturbations captured the direction of real responses, with up to 75% of perturbations shifted in the expected direction (scFoundation, p < 1e-8), and with its native overexpression protocol Geneformer V2 tracked CRISPRa effect sizes (ρ = 0.30, q = 0.001). Geneformer V2 networks recovered patient-level tumour differential expression better than co-expression (fold enrichment 2.9 versus 2.0). Within a species, most FM embeddings annotated cells as accurately as principal components. In organoids, predicted knockout effects were largely explained by where the target is expressed. Isogenic Canavan and Krabbe organoids and 4H patient organoids, with one library per line and time point, did not yet separate methods from differences between clones and donors, which defines the replication needed for future validation. Fine-tuning, including scFoundation embeddings in GEARS and Geneformer's own perturbation and disease protocols, changed classification accuracy but not knockout rankings, gene networks or the advantage of a training-mean baseline. We release the benchmark, baselines, pipeline checks and the isogenic reference task as the fmaudit package, so that new models can be evaluated on the tasks where FMs already help. 

## Key Points 

• Five single-cell foundation models, a GRN-based simulator, supervised perturbation models including STATE, and non-foundation baselines were compared on annotation, perturbation and GRN inference across cell lines, isogenic disease organoids, a public organoid CRISPR screen and human tumour biopsies. • Foundation models were most useful for transferring cell-type labels across species. From human to mouse Nicheformer exceeded every baseline, and fine-tuned Geneformer V2 did so from mouse to human. Within a species most methods performed similarly, and fine-tuned Geneformer V2 exceeded principal components on organoids. • Zero-shot in silico perturbations captured the direction of real K562 responses for several models, and with its native overexpression protocol Geneformer V2 also tracked CRISPRa effect sizes. In organoids predicted knockout effects largely followed target expression, and an expression-matched control test isolates the gene-specific part. • With patient-level ground truth, Geneformer V2 networks recovered tumour differential expression better than co-expression. ## Introduction 

Transformer models pre-trained on tens of millions of single-cell transcriptomes, such as scGPT [1], Geneformer [2], Nicheformer [3] and scFoundation [4], are presented as foundation models (FMs) that can be applied, with or without fine-tuning, to many downstream tasks. The tasks most often advertised are cell-type annotation, prediction of the transcriptional response to genetic perturbation, and inference of gene regulatory networks (GRNs). If reliable, FMs would enable in silico experiments in systems that are expensive to modify genetically, such as patient-derived organoids. Independent evaluations have tempered these expectations. For clustering and data integration, zero-shot FM embeddings often perform no better than highly variable genes or principal components [5,6]. For perturbation prediction, deep-learning models, FMs included, do not outperform deliberately simple linear or mean baselines [7,8], and zero-shot FM embeddings add little over simple baselines for perturbation effect prediction [9] and expression-forecasting methods rarely beat simple baselines [10]. These studies mostly evaluate one task in cell lines, where large Perturb-seq screens provide ground truth [11,12,13]. It remains unclear whether their conclusions extend to tissue-like systems and to the disease models in which in silico perturbation would be most useful, and whether a model that performs well on one task or system can be trusted on another. Here we evaluate the same methods on three tasks (zero-shot annotation, in silico perturbation and GRN inference) at three levels of biological complexity. These are (i) cell lines and primary cells, including K562 Perturb-seq screens, (ii) brain organoids from healthy donors (HD), isogenic Canavan (ASPA) and Krabbe (GALC) organoids with their parental lines, 4H leukodystrophy (POLR3A) patient organoids and a public CRISPR screen of 36 genes, and (iii) biopsies of EGFR-mutant lung cancer. The isogenic organoids provide a rare ground truth, the effect of a knockout in an otherwise identical background, if it can be separated from differences between clones, donors and libraries. We therefore simulated each knockout in the parental cells, treated libraries as replicates and included negative contrasts without a mutation. Errors in sample identity, data scaling or model training are easy to introduce and rarely visible in the results. We therefore applied systematic checks to every dataset and analysis step: genotype-based verification of sample identity, input-scale checks, verification of weight changes after fine-tuning, train/test overlap checks and fixed model inputs for in silico knockouts. These checks and the negative contrasts changed several conclusions, and we release them in the fmaudit package. 

## Methods 

Benchmark design 

The benchmark is a grid of tasks (annotation, perturbation, GRN) by datasets at three levels of biological complexity (Figure 1). Methods were scGPT [1], Geneformer V1-10M [2] and V2-316M, Nicheformer [3] and scFoundation [4], used zero-shot with the input processing of their own tutorials and, where applicable, after fine-tuning. We also ran CellOracle [14] with a promoter base GRN, optionally augmented with STRING v12 interactions [15], the supervised perturbation models GEARS [16], CPA [17], scGPT fine-tuned for perturbation and STATE [18], and non-FM baselines chosen per task, including gene-independent predictors such as the number of cells per cell type. All methods in a grid row were run on the same cells, split, gene universe and metric. Full protocols are in the Supplementary Methods.

Level 1, cell lines and primary cells: the Immune Human atlas (33,506 cells, 16 types) [19], Tabula Muris Senis lung (mouse) [20], and the Norman CRISPRa (91,205 cells) [11] and Adamson CRISPRi (68,603 cells, 87 conditions) [12] K562 Perturb-seq screens in GEARS processing. Level 2, brain organoids: organoids from five healthy donors (HD, 35,257 cells, 27 annotated cell types), including c107 and HD2.2, the parental lines of the isogenic lines. Canavan organoids came from three ASPA knockout clones of c107 (E27, E30 and E70, knockout validated by PCR, sequencing and western blot). Krabbe organoids came from a GALC knockout (KO) and a GALC G553R knock-in (KI) derived from HD2.2, and from a Krabbe patient-derived line. 4H leukodystrophy organoids came from two patients with POLR3A variants (variant details not available), without isogenic control. Disease organoids were annotated by label transfer from the HD organoids. Each line was sequenced as one library at day 100 and one at day 150 (Supplementary Table S4). HNOCA [21] served as annotation reference. Donor identity and sex of every library were checked from expressed SNPs and chromosome Y. Level 2 included the public CHOOSE mosaic organoid screen [22], in which perturbed and control cells share the same organoids. Level 3, human tissue: EGFR-mutant non-small-cell lung cancer biopsies [23], with the HLCA core [24] as human lung reference. 

Annotation 

scGPT used the continual-pretrained whole-human checkpoint and, for organoids, also the original whole-human and brain checkpoints. Zero-shot cell embeddings were used for k-nearest-neighbour label transfer (k = 30 and cosine distance within a dataset, k = 20 and Euclidean distance with a confidence override across datasets). Cross-species transfer used one-to-one orthologs in both directions and a coarse crosswalk of shared classes, and was repeated with cosine kNN (k = 30, no override) as a classifier control. Baselines were PCA on 2,000 highly variable genes with the same classifier, z-scored highly variable genes and CellTypist [25] trained on the reference. Organoids were scored as broad-lineage concordance with the manual annotation. 

In silico perturbation 

Zero-shot knockouts followed the embed, delete and re-embed protocol: the target gene was removed from each cell's input, and the effect was the cosine distance between original and perturbed embeddings, averaged per cell type. For scGPT the sampled input genes and tie-breaks were fixed per cell, so that the two inputs differed only by the target. In organoids we perturbed 14 genes (Supplementary Table S27) in a random sample of 5,000 HD cells. For the isogenic comparisons ASPA was deleted in all 7,336 c107 cells and GALC in all 6,999 HD2.2 cells, so that prediction and ground truth share the genetic background. A deletion changes the input only in cells that express the target: ASPA was detected in 69 of 7,336 c107 cells (0.94%, 9 of 27 cell types), GALC in 725 of 6,999 HD2.2 cells (10.4%, all cell types) and POLR3A in 26.1% of HD cells (all cell types, Supplementary Table S96). The GALC knock-in carries c.1657G>A (p.Gly553Arg), associated with infantile Krabbe disease [26] and without enzyme activity, consistent with misfolding [27,28,29], so it was compared with the same deletion as the knockout. In K562, each single-gene perturbation in a model's vocabulary was applied to 500 control cells, and Geneformer was also run with its own protocol, which models CRISPRa by moving the target to the first rank of the input. Supervised models were scored by Pearson δ on unseen GEARS test perturbations against training-mean and additive baselines [7]. GEARS, scGPT fine-tuned and GEARS with frozen scFoundation embeddings, the protocol of [4], were also trained on CHOOSE with five-fold gene hold-out, and Geneformer with its own perturbation-classifier protocol. Because cells of one library are not independent replicates [30], the isogenic ground truth was computed on pseudobulk counts per library and cell type (at least 10 cells) with edgeR quasi-likelihood [31] and the design ~ day + genotype, from raw counts on a common gene set. The primary target, fixed before scoring, was the summed absolute log2 fold change per cell type. All three scenarios are reported: GALC KO, KI and the patient-derived line versus HD2.2 (one residual degree of freedom), ASPA, and 4H (two POLR3A patients, four libraries, versus five healthy donors, ten libraries, ~ day + disease). For 4H, POLR3A was the target and POLR3B, a subunit of the same polymerase that is not mutated in these patients, served as a gene-level negative

The same pipeline gave negative contrasts without a mutation: clone E30 versus E27, and HD1.2, C16, HVS-420 and c107 versus HD2.2. A prediction was called knockout-specific if its Spearman ρ over cell types was positive with p < 0.05 and exceeded its highest ρ against the negatives by at least 0.2 (for 4H, also the ρ of the POLR3B prediction). ASPA and GALC KO were combined with a sign-aware Fisher test (one-sided p for ρ > 0). Naive predictors were the target's mean expression and detection rate, and the number of cells and median detected genes per cell type. Disease biology was characterised by preranked GSEA on MSigDB Hallmarks and Enrichr GO over-representation [32] from whole-organoid pseudobulk statistics. The CHOOSE screen [22] was analysed from the authors' processed object (28,344 cells, 36 genes, 16 cell types), with FDR-corrected DEG counts and a library-stratified Mantel-Haenszel composition test per gene and cell type as ground truth and knockouts simulated in control cells. For the lung-cancer biopsies [23] (23,261 Smart-seq2 cells), the ground truth was the number of DEGs per cell type for residual disease versus treatment-naive biopsies, per cell and, as a patient-aware control, by pseudobulk edgeR, with progression versus treatment-naive as specificity control. In both datasets library size and genes detected per cell type served as complexity baselines. 

Gene regulatory networks 

For each FM, gene-gene networks were derived from contextual gene-embedding similarity and from attention averaged over layers and heads, and compared with CellOracle, Pearson co-expression, an expression prior and random ranking. The top-50 neighbours of each hub gene were scored against knockdown targets (Adamson) and ChIP-seq targets (ChIP-Atlas [33]) in K562, genes responding to EGFR inhibition in treatment-naive cancer cells of the lung-cancer biopsies and, provisionally, CellOracle and STRING targets in organoids, as fold enrichment over an expression-matched expectation (FE@50, 1 = chance), pooled over hubs. The same networks were derived from fine-tuned backbones, including each FM adapted to K562 with the hub perturbations held out. 

Fine-tuning 

Each FM was fine-tuned once as a 27-class organoid cell-type classifier with its original pipeline (grid fine-tuning, FT): one stage for Geneformer (layers 4 and 5, embeddings and head), two for Nicheformer and scFoundation (head on the frozen backbone, then the last two layers with the head). scGPT was fine-tuned end-to-end for perturbation on Norman or Adamson. Geneformer, Nicheformer and scFoundation were also trained under one protocol in three arms (linear probe, partial unfreezing and LoRA, see Supplementary Methods). Hyper-parameters were then selected on validation cells within the training set (226 configurations), with the test cells used once. Each FM was also fine-tuned as a classifier on every annotation reference (4,000 training and 1,000 validation cells) against baselines tuned on the same cells. Following Geneformer's disease-modelling protocol, V1 and V2 were also fine-tuned to separate disease from control cells, holding out one day, and used to test whether deleting the target moved healthy cells toward the disease state, with healthy-donor classifiers as negatives. Knockouts were recomputed with each fine-tuned backbone. The scFoundation grid backbone, not saved, was re-trained with the same code for the parental-cell knockouts (Supplementary Table S34). 

Expression-matched specificity test 

To test whether a knockout carries information about the deleted gene beyond the loss of expressed input, the embedding shift after deleting target g in a cell was compared with the shifts after deleting, one at a time, 20 control genes matched to g in that cell for expression and across the dataset for detection rate. The specific effect is S = observed − mean(control) and z = S / sd(control). A gene×cell-type pair with z > 2 has a specific excess shift. Geneformer was also run with random controls.

## Results 

A task-by-system map with baselines 

Figure 1 summarises all task×dataset rows and method columns, each with a non-FM baseline computed on the same split and metric. In most rows the best FM was close to the baseline or below it. The exception was cross-species annotation, where some FMs clearly exceeded the baselines. 

Annotation: foundation models help mainly across species 

On primary immune cells, zero-shot kNN transfer from FM embeddings reached accuracies of 0.887 (Geneformer), 0.912 (scGPT), 0.926 (Nicheformer) and 0.949 (scFoundation). PCA with the same classifier reached 0.945 and CellTypist 0.921 (Figure 2A). On Tabula Muris lung, PCA (0.945, macro-F1 0.844) exceeded all FMs (0.839 to 0.902, macro-F1 0.635 to 0.765, Figure 2C, Supplementary Figure S1). In HD organoids all methods scored 0.54 to 0.65 (PCA 0.632), with macro-F1 ≤ 0.33. The original whole-human scGPT checkpoint reached 0.649, above PCA, and the brain checkpoint 0.622 (Supplementary Table S104). Mapping the lung-cancer biopsies onto the HLCA, FMs (0.91 to 0.94, except Geneformer V1 at 0.605) were close to PCA (0.924) and CellTypist (0.913). Across species results were model-dependent (Supplementary Figure S2). From human to mouse, Nicheformer (0.968), Geneformer V2 (0.914) and scFoundation (0.898) exceeded CellTypist with default settings (0.684) and cosine-kNN baselines (0.863), but CellTypist with regularisation selected on validation cells reached 0.941 (macro-F1 0.883), so only Nicheformer remained above every baseline. From mouse to human, no FM was separable from cosine-kNN PCA (0.945). Only Nicheformer was pre-trained on mouse cells too (Supplementary Table S9). Fine-tuned classifiers raised macro-F1 over zero-shot kNN within Tabula Muris lung for all five models (by 0.09 to 0.20), but only Geneformer V2 exceeded the best baseline trained on the same cells (0.892 versus 0.868). Across species fine-tuning lowered human-to-mouse macro-F1 below zero-shot for every model, whereas Geneformer V2 improved mouse-to-human transfer from 0.789 to 0.879, above every same-split baseline (at most 0.736, Supplementary Figure S9, Supplementary Table S108). 

Perturbation in cell lines: zero-shot models do not predict effect size 

For every representable single-gene perturbation of Norman and Adamson (75 to 152 per dataset), the predicted deletion magnitude was unrelated to the real effect size: Spearman's ρ between predicted cosine shift and the number of real DEGs ranged from −0.08 to 0.06 for the four FMs, none significant, and ranking perturbations by target expression did no better (ρ = −0.03 and 0.06, Figure 3A). The direction of the predicted shift carried more information. A deletion should move cells away from a CRISPRa response, and scFoundation did so for 75% of Norman perturbations (sign test p < 10−8). For Adamson knockdowns scGPT (70%, p = 0.0009) and Nicheformer (71%, p = 0.0002) shifted cells in the real direction (Figure 3B, Supplementary Tables S6 and S17). With Geneformer's own protocol, which models CRISPRa as overexpression, Geneformer V2 predicted Norman effect sizes (ρ = 0.30, p = 0.0002, BH q = 0.001) whereas V1 did not (ρ = −0.04, Figure 3A, Supplementary Table S106). Supervised models were compared with simple baselines on unseen test perturbations (Figure 3C). On Norman, GEARS reached a mean Pearson δ of 0.543 and scGPT fine-tuned 0.568, while predicting the mean training shift reached 0.557. For unseen single genes the baseline was higher than GEARS (0.498 vs 0.409), and for combinations of two seen genes the additive baseline was higher (0.887 vs 0.658), consistent with [7]. On Adamson, the training-mean baseline (0.711) exceeded GEARS (0.688), scGPT fine-tuned (0.642) and CPA (0.375), and was better than GEARS for 19 of the 22 test perturbations (Wilcoxon p = 0.0009, Supplementary Figure S3). Perturbation in organoids: predicted effects mostly track gene expression 

In HD organoids, the per-cell-type effect of knocking out a gene was strongly correlated across methods (ρ = 0.92 between Nicheformer and CellOracle), largely because the target's mean expression per cell type correlated with the predicted effect at ρ = 0.97 for CellOracle, 0.95 for Nicheformer, 0.90 for Geneformer V2, 0.85 for Geneformer, 0.79 for scGPT and 0.76 for scFoundation (378 gene×cell-type pairs, Figure 4A). With scGPT's original stochastic input, effects were almost uniform (ρ = 0.04) because re-sampling the input genes moved the embedding as much as the deletion (Supplementary Table S32). Fine-tuning as a classifier increased predicted effects up to three-fold while conserving their ranking (ρ = 0.93 to 0.96, Supplementary Figure S7E). 

The fine-tuning regime changes accuracy, not knockout predictions 

Under the uniform three-arm protocol (Supplementary Table S34), partial unfreezing and LoRA rescaled predicted effects while preserving their ranking (ρ = 0.90 to 0.98 with zero-shot), and no arm improved agreement with the isogenic ground truth (all Fisher p ≥ 0.080). Validation-based hyper-parameter selection changed test macro-F1 by −0.025 to +0.034 (Supplementary Table S107). On the same split, logistic regression on principal components (accuracy 0.705 to 0.716) exceeded Geneformer V1 and Nicheformer and matched scFoundation, and only Geneformer V2 with LoRA exceeded it (0.742, McNemar p = 0.013, macro-F1 difference +0.027, 95% CI −0.011 to 0.065, Supplementary Figure S8). No tuned backbone was knockout-specific. Geneformer classifiers trained to separate disease from control cells recognised GALC KO cells across days as well as principal components did (V2 AUROC 0.92 to 0.95), but separated two healthy donors at least as well (up to 0.996), and deleting the target did not move healthy cells toward the disease state more than toward another donor (lowest q = 0.24, Supplementary Table S111). Fine-tuned scGPT matched principal components on organoids (macro-F1 0.686 versus 0.692). 

Isogenic validation: no model separates knockout effects from background 

With one library per line and time point, the real knockout effects did not clearly exceed differences between clones and donors: ASPA KO versus c107 gave 1,777 DEGs over 20 cell types and clone E30 versus E27 gave 1,600, and GALC KO (2,983) and KI (2,439) fell within the range of healthy donors versus HD2.2 (1,519 to 16,856, Supplementary Tables S41 to S44). With knockouts simulated in the parental cells, no model correlated with the ASPA effect (ρ = −0.32 to 0.30, all p ≥ 0.17) or the GALC KO effect (ρ ≤ 0.06, 15 cell types), and none was knockout-specific in any of the four comparisons (Figure 4C). No Fisher combination of ASPA and GALC KO was significant (lowest p = 0.30, Figure 4D), no test survived correction (lowest q = 0.59, 52 tests), and the only positive association was the gene-independent number of cells per cell type (ρ = 0.50 for ASPA, Fisher p = 0.006). A cell-level ground truth followed cell number (ρ = 0.88 for GALC KO), which explains an apparent scFoundation association with GALC KO (ρ = 0.44, 0.10 after controlling for it, Supplementary Figure S7). ASPA, absent from 18 of 27 cell types and acting mainly in mature oligodendrocytes [35], left most predictions at zero. Every model predicted a larger mean effect for GALC than for ASPA, whereas both real knockouts were close to their negative contrasts (Supplementary Table S97). ASPA therefore tests a structural limit rather than predictive ability. The third scenario, 4H, has a broadly expressed target, POLR3A, but no isogenic control. Against the five healthy donors, the two POLR3A patients differed by 645 DEGs over 22 cell types. A larger model version improves annotation but not perturbation 

We repeated every Geneformer row with the V2-316M release (Supplementary Table S6). V2 annotated better than V1 on immune cells, Tabula Muris lung, the biopsies and from human to mouse (0.914 versus 0.642), but not zero-shot on HD organoids (0.532 versus 0.625). Zero-shot deletion results did not change: in organoids V2 effects followed target expression more closely than V1 (ρ = 0.90 versus 0.85), and neither version tracked the isogenic ASPA (V2 ρ = −0.08, V1 0.26) or GALC KO effects (−0.29, −0.40). The V2 network stayed near chance against K562 knockdown targets (FE@50 1.02). Scale therefore helped annotation and, with the overexpression protocol, CRISPRa effect sizes. 

Knockout effects are mostly non-specific 

The expression-matched specificity test explains why predicted effects follow target expression (Figure 8). Across 378 gene×cell-type pairs, the observed effect correlated with the target's mean expression at ρ = 0.65 (scGPT with seeded but not fixed input, 0.79 with fixed input, Supplementary Table S101), 0.81 (Geneformer), 0.93 (Nicheformer), 0.81 (scFoundation) and 0.97 (CellOracle). For scGPT and Geneformer this came from how many cells express the gene: over expressing cells only, the effect no longer tracked expression (ρ −0.09 to 0.09). Compared with deleting 20 control genes of the same expression in the same cell, deleting the target produced a specific excess shift (z > 2) in a minority of pairs: 20.5% for scGPT, 27.9% for Geneformer, 0.5% for Nicheformer, 35.3% for scFoundation and 11.7% for CellOracle. Removing the non-specific component did not improve agreement with real knockouts. In K562, the specific effect correlated with the number of DEGs or the energy distance at ρ −0.16 to 0.17 (all p ≥ 0.13). In the isogenic organoids neither raw nor specific effects tracked ASPA or GALC KO beyond the negative contrasts (all positive associations p ≥ 0.07, Figure 8B). In the CHOOSE screen, deleting the target shifted embeddings as much as deleting an expression-matched control gene (median ratio 1.00 for scGPT, 0.98 for Geneformer). 

Isogenic organoids: disease phenotypes 

Against the isogenic controls at matched days (Figure 5, Supplementary Figure S4), oligodendrocytes were rare in both control lines (1 to 19 cells per library), so lineage proportions are tentative. GALC KO did not deplete the lineage, GALC KI organoids contained more OPCs, and ASPA KO did not differ from c107. With library-level statistics, GALC KO, KI and patient organoids shared up-regulation of oxidative phosphorylation (NES 2.96, 2.49 and 3.16), TNF-α/NF-κB and mTORC1 hallmarks, whereas ASPA KO showed the same hallmarks down-regulated against c107 (oxidative phosphorylation NES −2.73) with no whole-organoid DEG. Myelination terms were not enriched. The opposite signs against the two control lines suggest differences between lines and libraries as much as genotype, so we do not interpret these signatures mechanistically. 

Gene regulatory networks: co-expression is a strong baseline 

Across K562, organoids and biopsies, FM networks recovered known regulatory targets close to chance (Figure 6). For Adamson knockdown targets, the best expression-matched FE@50 was 1.32 (scFoundation attention), equal to the expression prior (1.31) and below co-expression (1.61). For K562 ChIP-seq targets the best FM network (scGPT attention, 2.20) was also below co-expression (2.87). In the biopsies, the best FM network (scGPT embedding, 1.41) matched co-expression (1.40), and with patients as the unit of analysis (pseudobulk, nominal p < 0.01) the expression-matched Geneformer V2 network exceeded it (FE@50 2.90 versus 2.04), although on few hubs (Supplementary Figure S6, Supplementary Table S90). Fine-tuning did not change this: networks from backbones fine-tuned for perturbation, organoid classification or K562 perturbation identity did not exceed co-expression on any primary ground truth, and perturbation fine-tuning lowered scGPT attention enrichment for knockdown targets (0.62 on the two held-out Adamson hubs, zero-shot 2.04, Supplementary Table S112).

A public mosaic screen: apparent wins track cell complexity 

In the CHOOSE screen of 36 autism-associated genes in telencephalic organoids [22] composition can be tested within libraries. On the primary composition metric (|Mantel-Haenszel log odds ratio| per cell type), no FM and not CellOracle exceeded the naive expression predictors. On the number of significant DEGs per cell type (21 genes), Geneformer V2 (ρ = 0.22, p = 0.02) and scGPT (0.11, p = 0.048) exceeded the naive predictors, but neither survived correction over 32 tests (q ≥ 0.28), Geneformer V2 effects followed the genes detected per cell type (ρ = 0.92). Only CellOracle's gene-level composition score over its six simulable genes survived correction (ρ = 0.96, q = 0.045). Trained on the screen itself with five-fold gene hold-out, GEARS, scFoundation with GEARS and scGPT fine-tuned predicted held-out genes (Pearson δ 0.27, 0.33 and 0.54 on the top 20 DE genes) below the mean training response (0.68), and Geneformer fine-tuned as a perturbation classifier did not beat its zero-shot version (median ρ −0.05 versus 0.05 for V1, 0.20 versus 0.28 for V2) or a permuted-label control (0.20 for V2, Supplementary Table S110). Cell complexity is thus a further baseline to report (Supplementary Figure S5, Supplementary Table S103). 

Human EGFR-TKI lung cancer: patient effects dominate cell-level ground truth 

In biopsies of EGFR-mutant lung cancer before targeted therapy (TN, 7 patients) and at residual disease (RD, 8 patients) [23], every method placed the cancer compartment first after an in silico EGFR knockout, and so did the naive EGFR-expression predictors (Figure 7). Against cell-level RD-versus-TN DEG counts all methods correlated positively (ρ = 0.20 to 0.53), but the progression contrast (PD versus TN) gave equal values for scGPT (0.52) and CellOracle (0.53). With patients as the unit (pseudobulk), seven of eight methods correlated negatively with the ground truth. The cell-level agreement thus reflects differences between patients and biopsies rather than treatment (Supplementary Figure S6). 

## Discussion 

Evaluated against simple baselines on the same data, current single-cell FMs helped in specific settings: mapping a reference across species, the direction of perturbation responses, CRISPRa effect sizes with Geneformer's own overexpression protocol and, with patient-level ground truth, tumour regulatory networks. Within a species their embeddings support label transfer about as well as principal components, as reported for zero-shot clustering [5,6], although fine-tuned Geneformer V2 exceeded them on organoids and Tabula Muris lung. Across species the margin over CellTypist depended on tuning its regularisation, which a fair benchmark must do. Their zero-shot in silico knockouts do not predict how strongly a real knockout perturbs a cell line and, in organoids, largely restate where the target gene is expressed. Their gene-gene networks do not recover knockdown targets or ChIP-seq binding better than co-expression. These results extend earlier conclusions from cell lines [5,6,7,8] to organoids and tumour biopsies. In the isogenic organoids, with one library per line and time point the knockout effect could not be separated from differences between clones or donors. The three organoid scenarios show what an in silico knockout needs in order to be tested: an expressed target, a controlled genetic background and replicates, and no comparison had all three. ASPA, with the best replicates, marks a structural limit: a deletion changes a cell's input only where the gene is detected, and its cells of action are rare at this stage. POLR3A shows that expression alone is not sufficient without a controlled background. Apparent successes depended on how the ground truth was built. Agreement between methods was here mostly shared dependence on target expression, which a naive predictor reproduces. The specificity test gives a mechanism for this dependence and a practical control. Deleting a gene from a cell's input changes the embedding roughly as much as deleting any other gene of similar expression, so the per-cell-type effect mostly measures how many cells express the target. Only a minority of gene×cell-type pairs exceed an expression-matched null, and the excess does not predict real knockout outcomes. We propose that in silico perturbation results be reported against such a null. The test is implemented in fmaudit.

Fine-tuning did not change this picture. Neither validation-based hyper-parameter selection nor Geneformer's disease-modelling protocol produced a knockout-specific prediction, and fine-tuned backbones did not yield better gene networks. Adapting the backbone rescaled predicted effects (about three-fold for Geneformer and Nicheformer) without changing their ranking, so it altered sensitivity rather than correctness. Disease classifiers separated healthy donors as readily as genotypes, so in these organoids donor and library identity dominate what a model learns. Supervised models trained on Perturb-seq or on the organoid screen itself, including GEARS with scFoundation embeddings, did not beat the mean training shift for unseen genes and cannot be applied to systems without perturbation data, such as patient organoids. Without the checks applied here, a control matrix normalised twice would have altered the ground truth for all GALC comparisons. A fine-tuning run that silently fell back to the pretrained model would have suggested that fine-tuning leaves predictions unchanged. An input re-sampled before and after each deletion would have made scGPT's effects unrelated to the deleted gene. A cell-level ground truth would have rewarded predictors of cell number. Running Nicheformer without its species and assay tokens changed its values, although not the conclusions (Supplementary Table S105). Table 1 summarises the resulting recommendations, including a target-detection check now included in fmaudit, for researchers who want to apply FMs to their own data. Limitations: each organoid line was sequenced as one library per time point, so the isogenic tests have few replicates and 15 to 20 cell types each, and replicate differentiations are needed before isogenic organoids can rank models. In silico deletion also represents only cell-autonomous effects. The 4H comparison is not isogenic, and the patients' POLR3A variants, which were not available to us, may reduce rather than abolish function, so deletion is an approximation. FMs see only expression, so the G553R knock-in, whose transcript is normally expressed (median log2 ratio −0.18), was modelled as a deletion, and missense variants will require models that combine expression with sequence. The grid inputs of Geneformer were ranked from log-normalised values rather than raw counts, which its native-protocol analyses corrected. Organoid GRN references lack orthogonal ground truth, and the biopsies come from few patients. Newer models may behave differently.

## Conclusions 

Across annotation, perturbation and GRN inference, and from cell lines to isogenic disease organoids, a public organoid screen and tumour biopsies, single-cell FMs were matched by simple baselines in most comparisons, and no model predicted the effects of real knockouts in isogenic organoids beyond negative contrasts. Isogenic disease models can provide the ground truth that in silico perturbation needs, provided that they include replicate libraries and negative contrasts. Together with task-matched baselines and pipeline checks, they should become a standard component of FM benchmarks.

Figure 8. Expression-matched specificity test for in silico knockout. (A) Distribution of z (observed shift minus the mean of 20 expression-matched control deletions, divided by their standard deviation) per gene×cell-type pair in HD organoids, with the fraction of pairs above z = 2. Geneformer V1 with random controls is shown for comparison, and Geneformer V2 next to V1. STATE is not applicable because its knockout is a perturbation token rather than an input deletion. (B) Spearman ρ of raw (open) and specific (filled) effects with target-gene expression in organoids, with the pseudobulk isogenic ground truth of ASPA KO (6 to 20 cell types, as specific effects need at least three expressing cells) and GALC KO (15 cell types), with grey bars for the range over the design-matched negative contrasts, and with the number of differentially expressed genes per perturbation in K562 (Norman, Adamson).
