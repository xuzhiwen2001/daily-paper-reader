Abstract 

Counterfactual editing of single cells, predicting how an observed cell would look had one factor been different, enables in-silico screening where experiments are infeasible, but is useful only if the edit is faithful to the individual and not merely to the condition. Concept bottleneck generative mod-els make the effects of interventions interpretable by decomposing a cell’s representation into known and unknown factors. Previous work uses a linear cross-covariance penalty to disentangle known and unknown dimensions. We demonstrate that this first-order criterion does not fully separate information when non-linear interactions are present. We first demonstrate the inadequacy of linear cross-factor pe-nalization with simulation experiments with controlled edits and known non-linear covariate effects. We then demonstrate that adding a nonlinear kernel criterion, the normalized Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion, is able to recover the true intervention effects on simulation data. Finally, we apply the non-linear concept bottleneck model to a complex single-cell RNA-seq dataset and find that it improves the ability to generate counterfactual expression profiles of cells under different tissue environments. 

# 1 Introduction 

Counterfactual modeling asks what an observation would have looked like had one of its underlying factors been different when other factors are held fixed. The dominant framework is Pearl’s abduction–action– prediction [1]: infer the latent state that explains the observation, intervene on a single factor, predict the outcome. The edit is useful only if it is at once effective , it produces the requested change, and faithful ,it alters nothing else. These models are particularly valuable to observational samples in biology, where high-dimensional measurements are assumed to lie in a lower dimensional space, exhaustive sampling of potential conditions is expensive and infeasible, and a sample can usually only be measured once with a single modality. Common biological counterfactual questions include what a cell’s gene expression would be in a different environment, e.g. different tissue, or under a different context, e.g. alternative drug treat-ment. Single-cell RNA sequencing [2], measuring the gene expression of individual cells [3] from a sample, provides increasing resolution into disease biology at ever increasing cell numbers and resolution [4, 5]. Improved throughput and decreasing costs have enabled the production of reference atlases across tissues 1arXiv:2611.13748v1 [cs.LG] 10 Nov 2026 and diseases [6, 7, 8, 9, 10], and enabled training of latent-variable models of the cell types and states [11]. However, while modern foundation models pretrained on tens of millions of cells [12, 13] can describe cel-lular states, they have fallen short on valuable counterfactual prediction. Improved counterfactual models of single-cell RNA-seq data would enable the exploration of the combinatorial space of cell types, patients, treatments and doses that is infeasible at current experiment scales. Existing models of cellular response follow two overlapping directions. The first predicts intervention effects at the level of populations rather than of a given cell, whether by assuming a fixed additive shift in latent space [14, 15], conditioning a decoder on the perturbation [16], propagating it through a prior knowledge graph [17], or mapping unpaired control and treated populations onto one another by optimal transport [18, 19]; the transport maps do act per cell, but they match distributions rather than reconstruct a named factor. The second uses interpretable representations with biologically named components [20]. Several methods combine these directions through latent-space arithmetic over disentangled factors [21, 22], latent optimization with capacity-constrained unknown-attribute embeddings [23], or causally struc-tured concept embeddings decoded by a diffusion model [24]. All emit a profile per input cell, but their evaluations do not explicitly test whether an edit preserves donor-specific identity or matches the source donor’s response under the target condition. The concept bottleneck generative model scCBGM combines both approaches [25] by implementing the concept bottleneck idea [26] for a generative model of cells. The model decomposes each cell’s latent representation into a supervised block of observed concepts, such as cell type, tissue or treatment, and a set of unknown factors that account for other unrecorded variation, from donor identity to library size and batch. Counterfactual predictions for the known factors can be generated via abduction–action–prediction: encode a cell, replace one concept with its target value, and decode from the same unknown factors. Because the concepts are explicit, the edit can be checked rather than trusted, which makes it a plausible route to person-alized prediction. This counterfactual, however, is only valid if the two blocks are genuinely independent, such that editing a concept does not alter another concept’s effect, e.g. a donor-specific signal that makes the prediction personal. To ensure disentanglement of the latent factors and concepts, scCBGM enforces independence with a penalty on the linear cross-covariance between the blocks, a first-order criterion blind to any dependence whose conditional means agree. Kernel criteria are the known remedy in this setting: Lopez et al. constrained a single-cell VAE with the d-variable Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion to keep the latent independent of observed nuisance factors [27]. Here both arguments are learned, the concept head on one side and the unknown block on the other, and the question is not invariance to a nuisance but whether an edit made through the concept pathway stays faithful to the individual carried in the unknowns. We show that the linear penalty is not always enough. On a matched pair of simulations differing in exactly one place, the cross-covariance penalty recovers donor-specific counterfactuals when the edited condition enters as an additive mean shift shared across donors, and fails at every penalty strength when it instead modulates the variance of a donor-specific signature, a regime in which the dependence between the blocks is entirely second-order and the penalty reports no violation. Adding a kernel criterion, the normalized Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion [28], recovers the donor-specific edit, and the same ordering holds on the TAURUS inflammatory bowel disease cohort [29], where the added kernel penalty produces an accurate rectum → ileum edit.

We ask whether a concept bottleneck generative model edits a cell in a donor-specific way. The original single cell concept bottleneck paper established that flipping such a concept can move cells into the target condition [25]. To that end we implemented a simulator in which the donor effect is known by construction, so that the correct donor-specific answer is available as ground truth even for donors the model has never seen. The simulation uses a hierarchical Poisson model to generate single-cell like data [25] (Sec. 4.2). ## 2.2 Donor effects must be low rank for held-out donors to be predictable 

In the original single cell concept bottleneck simulator, the donor fingerprint γb,g, the effect of donor b on gene g, is drawn i.i.d. per gene, which makes each donor an isolated point in gene space: nothing learned from the training donors carries information about an unseen donor’s vector γb, and the donor holdout is unsolvable in principle rather than merely hard. To properly model donor-specific effects, we replace the donor block by a shared factor model γ = HW with H ∈ RB×R and W ∈ RR×F (Eq. 8), where B = 150 is the number of donors, F = 3,000 the number of genes, and R = 12 the rank: H gives each donor R loadings and 

W holds R gene programs shared across donors, the analogue of a small number of transcriptional programs that individuals up- or downregulate to different degrees rather than F independent per-gene effects. The program matrix W is drawn once and shared, and its variance is scaled by 1 /R so that the per-gene donor variance Var (γb,g) = σ 2

The useful range is therefore bounded on both sides, and the two failure modes look nothing alike.

# 3 Discussion 

We asked whether a concept bottleneck generative model edits a cell in a donor-specific way, and the answer depends on how the edited condition enters the data. On a matched pair of simulations with linear and non-linear donor specific effects, the linear cross-covariance penalty of scCBGM [25] suffices when the condition is an additive mean shift shared by all donors and fails when it instead modulates the variance of a donor-specific signature, regardless of the strength of the linear orthogonality penalty. The failure is not an absent edit with a high enough penalty: the decoder moves the cells, but to a generic target-condition position rather than that donor’s own. Adding a kernel criterion, nHSIC, to the linear one recovers the donor-specific edit, and the same holds on Taurus [29], where the covariance-only numerators sit at the denominator while cov + nHSIC completes the edit. Neither regime is a strawman. The additive one is plausibly what the interferon-β stimulation benchmark of Kang et al. [30] provides, on which scCBGM was evaluated with the covariance penalty alone: a coordinated up-regulation of an interferon program largely shared across cells and donors, hence a first-order effect that a first-order criterion can keep out of U. The heteroskedastic regime is what one expects when a perturbation amplifies an individual’s existing program instead of adding 7Figure 3: Counterfactual edit of the held-out stem_LGR5pos subtype (rectum → terminal ileum) under three orthogonality penalties. Every is_ileum =ileum stem_LGR5pos cell ( n=4,100, green) was withheld from training; at inference the retained rectal cells of the same subtype ( n=6,342, blue) are edited to ileum (red) and compared against them. The low-rank donor structure is likewise biologically grounded, individuals differing mainly by up- or downregulating a limited set of shared transcriptional programs rather than through F independent per-gene effects, and it is what makes an unseen donor predictable at all. We therefore recommend adding the kernel penalty rather than replacing the linear one. The terms are complementary: cross-covariance is a cheap, low-variance estimate of the first-order dependence, whereas nHSIC must estimate a full kernel dependence from a few hundred cells, so keeping both anchors the first order and leaves the kernel term only what covariance cannot see. The combination reaches the best value in both regimes at the smallest weight (0 .03 and 0 .15 at λ⊥ = 0.5), where nHSIC alone needs λ⊥ = 5 to reach 0 .35. Hyperparameter sensitivity is the main current weakness of nonlinear concept bottleneck models. We find that over-regularization is a potential issue with the combined kernel and linear penalty. Tuning on rMMD alone would miss this, and we find that joint evaluation with the edit ratio and UMAPs is required. The best setting differed between our two datasets ( λ⊥ = 0.5 in simulation, 10 on Taurus). We did not explore the estimator’s batch-size dependence and its median-heuristic bandwidths. Our results are also single-seed, reported without intervals, live in a 128-dimensional PCA space under a Gaussian loss rather than in count space, and rest on one simulator and one cohort with a single held-out subtype. Growing longitudinal and pan-tissue collection will eventually make further evaluations and develop-ment feasible. As data increases, we can move towards patient, rather than cell level, prediction and genera-tion - treating each patient as a distribution over cells and the cohort as a distribution over those distributions, simultaneously generating cell type abundances and cell states. With such a readout the applications follow from the counterfactual framing, in-silico treatment screening in a specific patient, imputing a tissue that was never biopsied, mapping an individual along an axis such as age, and transferring a biomarker from accessible blood to the tissue where the disease sits. By con-struction

Class-balanced concept loss. The binary tissue concept is strongly imbalanced (83% non-ileum) in the Taurus dataset, and an unweighted BCE lets the head reach a high accuracy while under-fitting the minority level, which is exactly the concept we edit. We therefore rescale the positive entries of column j by w j =(1 − p j)/p j, with p j the training prevalence of that level, keeping reduction=’mean’ so the loss scale of the other concepts is unchanged. For the taurus runs this gives w = 4.88 on is_ileum ; the cell-type concept is left unweighted. The synthetic runs use the unweighted loss (all levels are near-balanced by construction). 

Inputs and concepts. Both settings enter the model in the same representation: median library-size nor-malization, log (1 + x), the top 3 ,000 highly variable genes ( seurat_v3 ), and PCA to 128 components fitted once on all cells, so d = 128. This means the whole model training and evaluations run in PCA space. Con-cepts are supervised one-hot blocks: taurus uses the 7-way major lineage label plus the binary is_ileum 

(dc = 8); the simulation uses the 4-way celltype plus the binary is_ileum (dc = 5). Donor identity and sub cell type are never concepts, but must be recovered in u, which is what the counterfactual evaluation tests. 

Splits. Two nested splits are used, with different purposes. (i) Evaluation holdout : Removed from training entirely, never seen in any form. For taurus this is a global subtype holdout : all cells of the held-out subtype in the target condition ( is_ileum = ileum × stem_LGR5pos ) are withheld, 4 ,100 of 987 ,743 cells, leaving 983 ,643 for training. At evaluation the retained Rectum stem_LGR5pos cells are edited non_ileum → ileum and compared to the real held-out ileal cells, per donor for the 14 donors that carry the subtype on both sides, and pooled. For the simulation this is a donor holdout : 20 of the 150 donors are withheld in full (207 ,231 of 240 ,000 cells remain), and the edit is scored per cell type on donors the model has never seen (Sec. 4.2). (ii) Train/validation : A uniformly random 10% of the remaining cells is held out as a validation set for monitoring, early stopping and checkpoint selection only: 885 ,279 / 98 ,364 for taurus 

and 186 ,508 / 20 ,723 for the simulation. This split is disjoint from (i) and is drawn from a fixed seed, so training and inference reconstruct identical splits from the observation table without caching them. 

Optimization. Adam [33], learning rate 3 × 10 −4 with an exponential per-epoch decay ( γ = 0.997), batch size 512 with reshuffling each epoch, at most 256 epochs. Training stops early when the total validation loss has not improved for patience consecutive epochs ( min_delta = 0), and the checkpoint with the lowest total validation loss, not the last one, is the model used for every reported evaluation. Gradients are clipped to a global norm of 1 .0 in the synthetic runs and left unclipped in the taurus runs. All runs use seed 0 for the data split, weight initialization, dropout and batch order.

Ratio metrics. Absolute distances are not comparable across subtypes or datasets, so, following [25], we normalize by the distance from the target to the closest population that already exists in the training data. A value below 1 means the edit is a better description of the target than the best available "map the cells onto the nearest existing population" answer; the numerator, the identity of the winning population p⋆ and the full candidate list are recorded for every run. The minimum is taken separately for each metric, so the winner may differ between rMMD and rSinkhorn. 

Candidate populations, pooled subtype holdout (Taurus). Here P is the set of joint combinations of the model’s own concept columns: every observed pair (major lineage, is_ileum ) among the training cells, with populations of fewer than 10 cells dropped. For the Taurus runs this yields 14 candidates from the 7 lineages crossed with the binary tissue concept. Fine subtypes are deliberately not used to define candidates, so the baseline is "the closest population the model is allowed to name", not "the same subtype in the target condition", which would not exist for a held-out subtype. For all three Taurus runs the winning population was (epithelial, non_ileum) at MMD 0 .244, i.e. the un-edited source lineage, and the same population also won under the Sinkhorn divergence. 

Candidate populations, donor holdout (simulation). The donor holdout scores each (held-out donor b,cell type k) pair separately. The numerator is the distance between the edit of that donor’s source cells of type k and the same donor’s real target cells of type k. The denominator is the distance between the generic 

training population of the target condition and cell type k, pooled over all training donors, and that same donor’s real target cells. The ratio therefore isolates donor specificity: a model that predicts the condition correctly but ignores who the donor is scores ≈ 1 by construction, and only a model that recovers the donor’s own position scores below it. A (donor, cell type) row is scored only if both sides have at least 20 cells and the denominator population has at least 10; we report the median over the surviving rows and, where stated, the fraction of rows below 1. For the final simulation pair this gives 65 rows across the 20 held-out donors. 

Subtype screen. The held-out subtype for the pooled evaluation was chosen before any training by a model-free screen. For each of the 36 location-agnostic fine subtypes we computed the real source-versus-target distance (MMD and Sinkhorn, per donor and pooled) together with the number of donors carrying the subtype on both sides and the cell counts per side. Subtypes whose two conditions barely differ are unsuitable, because the denominator of Eq. 20 then collapses onto the source population and the ratio be-comes unstable. stem_LGR5pos ranked third by per-donor MMD (0 .201; pooled 0 .146) with 14 paired donors, 12 ,614 source and 4 ,100 target cells, and was preferred over the two larger-gap subtypes for its higher paired-donor count and its coherence in the target condition. Because the holdout is baked into train-ing, each candidate subtype would require its own model, so the screen is run first and only the selected subtype’s hold-out model is trained.
