# Where single-cell foundation models help: a public-data benchmark of annotation, in silico perturbation and gene regulatory inference 

Francesca Cupaioli and Ivan Merelli* Institute for Biomedical Technologies, National Research Council (CNR-ITB), Via Fratelli Cervi 93, 20054 Segrate (MI), Italy *Corresponding author: Ivan Merelli, ivan.merelli@itb.cnr.it 

## Abstract 

Single-cell foundation models (FMs) are proposed as general tools for cell-type annotation, in silico perturbation and gene-regulatory-network inference. We benchmarked five FMs (scGPT, Geneformer V1 and V2, Nicheformer, scFoundation), four newer FMs, CellOracle, supervised perturbation models and task-matched simple baselines on public data, from Perturb-seq screens of cell lines and primary T cells to a brain organoid screen, the developing mouse cortex and lung cancer biopsies, under an analysis plan fixed before the new results. FMs helped most when labels were transferred across species: Nicheformer reached 0.98 accuracy from human to mouse lung, above every baseline (at most 0.94), and fine-tuned Geneformer V2 reached macro-F1 0.88 from mouse to human, against at most 0.74. In K562, zero-shot perturbations captured the direction of real responses (up to 76% of perturbations), Geneformer V2 tracked CRISPRa effect sizes with its overexpression protocol (ρ = 0.30), and its networks recovered tumour differential expression better than co-expression. Within a species, FM embeddings did not exceed principal components. In silico deletions followed target expression and, across seven screens with replicate-level ground truth, met the predefined criterion in none of 81 tests, although in K562 and RPE1 the measured effects replicated between libraries. In contrast, the gene vectors of seven of eight FMs, including three newer models, added to expression features, predicted which knockdowns have large effects in an independent genome-wide K562 screen (ρ between 0.34 and 0.44, against 0.26 with expression alone). We release the benchmark and its checks as the fmaudit package. 

## Key Points 

• Five single-cell foundation models, a GRN-based simulator, supervised perturbation models including STATE, and non-foundation baselines were compared on annotation, perturbation and GRN inference across cell lines, primary cells, genome-scale CRISPRi screens in primary T cells, a public organoid CRISPR screen, an in vivo mouse cortex screen and human tumour biopsies, all from public data. • Foundation models were most useful for transferring cell-type labels across species. From human to mouse Nicheformer exceeded every baseline, and fine-tuned Geneformer V2 did so from mouse to human. Within a species principal components matched or exceeded every model, also after fine-tuning. • Zero-shot in silico perturbations captured the direction of real K562 responses for several models, and with its native overexpression protocol Geneformer V2 also tracked CRISPRa effect sizes. In an organoid screen and in the mouse cortex, predicted knockout effects followed target expression and were no larger than those of expression-matched control genes. In genome-scale screens of primary CD4 T cells, K562 and RPE1, predicted effect sizes did not exceed target expression or the number of perturbed cells, although the measured effects replicated well in the two cell lines, and their shifts pointed towards the real response of the deleted gene only faintly (2 of 20 tests). The gene vectors that FMs learn, however (seven of eight models tested, including three newer ones), predicted which knockdowns have large effects beyond expression, as confirmed on an independent genome-wide K562 screen, and remained informative over STRING degree in a robustness analysis.

## Introduction 

Transformer models pre-trained on tens of millions of single-cell transcriptomes, such as scGPT [1], Geneformer [2], Nicheformer [3] and scFoundation [4], are presented as foundation models (FMs) that can be applied, with or without fine-tuning, to many downstream tasks, most often cell-type annotation, prediction of the transcriptional response to genetic perturbation, and inference of gene regulatory networks (GRNs). If reliable, FMs would enable in silico experiments in systems that are expensive to modify genetically, such as organoids and tissues. Independent evaluations have tempered these expectations. For clustering and data integration, zero-shot FM embeddings often perform no better than highly variable genes or principal components [5,6]. For perturbation prediction, deep-learning models, FMs included, do not outperform deliberately simple linear or mean baselines [7,8], and neither zero-shot FM embeddings [9] nor expression-forecasting methods [10] improve on them. These studies mostly evaluate one task in cell lines, where large Perturb-seq screens provide ground truth [11,12,13]. It remains unclear whether their conclusions extend to tissue-like systems and to the disease models in which in silico perturbation would be most useful, and whether a model that performs well on one task or system can be trusted on another. Here we evaluate the same methods on three tasks (annotation, in silico perturbation and GRN inference) at three levels of biological complexity, using only public data: (i) cell lines and primary cells, including Perturb-seq screens of K562 and RPE1 [13] and a genome-scale CRISPRi screen of primary human CD4 T cells [14], (ii) neural tissue, with the CHOOSE mosaic CRISPR screen of 36 genes in brain organoids [15] and an in vivo Perturb-seq screen of 35 autism risk genes in the developing mouse cortex [16], together with CRISPRi and CRISPRa screens of iPSC-derived neurons [17], and (iii) biopsies of EGFR-mutant lung cancer. Perturbation screens in tissue provide the ground truth that in silico perturbation needs, if the measured effect of a gene can be separated from differences between cell types and libraries. We therefore fixed the analysis plan before computing the new results, scored every prediction against naive predictors and permutation nulls within cell type, and estimated how well the measured effects replicate between libraries. Errors in data scaling or model training are easy to introduce and rarely visible in the results. We therefore checked every analysis step: input-scale checks, verification of weight changes after fine-tuning, train/test overlap checks, target-detection checks and fixed model inputs for in silico knockouts. These checks changed several conclusions, and we release them in the fmaudit package.

Datasets 

Level 1, cell lines and primary cells: the Immune Human atlas (33,506 cells, 16 types) [23], Tabula Muris Senis lung (mouse) [24], and the Norman CRISPRa (91,205 cells) [11] and Adamson CRISPRi (68,603 cells, 87 conditions) [12] K562 Perturb-seq screens in GEARS processing, the CRISPRi essential-gene Perturb-seq screens of K562 and RPE1 [13] from the scPerturb collection [25], and a genome-scale CRISPRi Perturb-seq screen of primary human CD4 T cells from four donors, at rest and after 8 and 48 hours of stimulation [14]. Level 2, neural tissue: the CHOOSE mosaic organoid screen [15] (28,344 cells, 36 perturbed genes, 991 non-targeting control cells, 16 cell types annotated by the authors), in which perturbed and control cells share the same organoids, with HNOCA [26] as annotation reference, the CRISPRi and CRISPRa screens of iPSC-derived neurons [17] from the scPerturb collection, and the in vivo Perturb-seq screen of 35 autism and neurodevelopmental risk genes in the developing mouse cortex [16] (GEO GSE157977, perturbations delivered in utero at E12.5, cells profiled at P7), with the authors' cell classes and perturbation assignments from the Single Cell Portal [27] (17,889 analysis cells, 17 libraries, five cell classes, 374 GFP control cells). Datasets and their processing are listed in Supplementary Table S1, and the design of the mouse cortex analysis in Supplementary Table S2. Level 3, human tissue: EGFR-mutant non-small-cell lung cancer biopsies [28], with the HLCA core [29] as human lung reference. 

Annotation 

scGPT used the continual-pretrained whole-human checkpoint and, for the organoid screen, also the original whole-human and brain checkpoints. Zero-shot cell embeddings were used for k-nearest-neighbour label transfer (k = 30 and cosine distance within a dataset, k = 20 and Euclidean distance with a confidence override across datasets). Cross-species transfer used one-to-one orthologs in both directions and a coarse crosswalk of shared classes, and was repeated with cosine kNN (k = 30, no override) as a classifier control. Cells left unassigned by the override were not scored (fractions and accuracy over all cells in Supplementary Table S3). Baselines were PCA on 2,000 highly variable genes with the same classifier, z-scored highly variable genes and CellTypist [30] trained on the reference. Organoid screen cells were annotated within the dataset (stratified 50/50 split) and, after mapping onto HNOCA, scored as broad-lineage concordance with the authors' annotation. 

In silico perturbation 

Zero-shot knockouts followed the embed, delete and re-embed protocol: the target gene was removed from each cell's input, and the effect was the cosine distance between original and perturbed embeddings, averaged per cell type. For scGPT the sampled input genes and tie-breaks were fixed per cell, so that the two inputs differed only by the target. A deletion changes the input only in cells that express the target, so target detection was checked per cell type before simulation. In K562, each single-gene perturbation in a model's vocabulary was applied to 500 control cells, and Geneformer was also run with its own protocol, which models CRISPRa by moving the target to the first rank of the input. In the organoid screen the 36 genes were deleted in all 991 non-targeting cells. In the mouse cortex the genes were deleted in the 374 GFP control cells from raw counts, through one-to-one human orthologs for the models trained on human data (32 of the 35 genes have an ortholog in our table) and with the mouse species token for Nicheformer. Supervised models were scored by Pearson δ on unseen GEARS test perturbations against training-mean and additive baselines [7]. GEARS, scGPT fine-tuned and GEARS with frozen scFoundation embeddings, the protocol of [4], were also trained on CHOOSE with five-fold gene hold-out, and Geneformer with

Because cells of one library are not independent replicates [31], measured effects in tissue were computed on pseudobulk counts per library and cell type with edgeR quasi-likelihood [32]. In the organoid screen the ground truth per gene and cell type was the number of DEGs at FDR < 0.05 and the absolute library-stratified Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel log odds ratio of composition (Supplementary Table S4). In the mouse cortex perturbed cells were compared with GFP control cells of the same class and library (paired design, at least 3 cells of each group per library and at least 20 perturbed cells over at least 2 libraries), giving 72 gene by class pairs, 65 of them with predictions, and, as a secondary reference, with all other perturbed cells of the same class and library (152 pairs, Supplementary Table S5). The measured effect was the number of DEGs at FDR < 0.05 and the summed absolute log2 fold change. The analysis plan, fixed before these results, called a prediction positive if its Spearman ρ over gene by cell-type pairs was positive with Benjamini-Hochberg q < 0.05 within its family, exceeded the best naive predictor by at least 0.10 with a bootstrap interval of the difference above 0, and exceeded a null in which perturbed genes were permuted within expression decile or within cell type (1,000 permutations). Naive predictors were the target's mean expression and detection rate, the number of perturbed and control cells, and library size and genes detected per cell type. The replication ceiling was the agreement of the measured effects between two halves of the libraries. Genome-scale screens. In the primary CD4 T cell screen [14], 600 targets were drawn by decile of target expression (seed 0) among the 5,217 targets with significant on-target knockdown and no off-target flag in all three culture conditions and scored in at least three donors, and deleted in 200 resting non-targeting cells (50 per donor). The measured effect was the authors' number of DEGs (DESeq2, 10% FDR) at rest and after 8 and 48 hours of stimulation, the stimulated conditions being scored with the resting predictions, and the replication ceiling was the agreement between donors of per-donor edgeR effects. In the scPerturb screens [25], 600 targets shared by K562 and RPE1 were drawn by K562 expression decile, and all targets were used in the neuron screens. The measured effect was the number of genes with Wilcoxon p < 0.01 among the 3,000 most variable genes, perturbed against library-matched control cells, from raw counts, and the replication ceiling was the agreement between two halves of the libraries. Predictions used 500 control cells (all 437 and 434 in the neuron screens, and 100 for Geneformer V2 and scFoundation in K562, RPE1 and neuron CRISPRa because of their cost). The decision rule, the naive predictors (target expression, detection rate, number of perturbed cells) and the permutation within expression decile were those of the tissue screens, with Benjamini-Hochberg correction over the 15 T cell and the 20 cell-line tests. GEARS and scGPT with GEARS were trained on resting T cells with five-fold target hold-out and on the GEARS-processed essential-gene screens with the GEARS split (412 and 651 perturbations whose target is among the 5,000 genes of the files). STATE was not scored on these screens because they are in its training data. Gene representations. The context-free gene vector of each FM (the input embedding of the gene, without positional embedding) was used as a feature of a ridge regression of the log number of DEGs of each target, alone or added to expression features (mean log expression and detection rate of the target in control cells, log number of perturbed cells) or to the STRING v12 degree of the target (combined score at least 700). Principal components (at most 50), standardisation and the penalty were fitted on training targets only. Transfer between systems used fixed halves of the shared targets, and analyses within a system used 5-fold (10-fold for the genome-wide screen) target cross-validation with pooled out-of-fold Spearman correlation. A code test on random truths showed that this procedure can produce gains of up to 0.05 by itself, so the gain over expression was tested against 200 null truths with permuted residuals of a least-squares fit on the expression features (Freedman and Lane), an amendment made before any score of the confirmatory screen [33]. The confirmatory screen was the genome-wide CRISPRi Perturb-seq of K562 [13] (7,568 targets with at least 25 perturbed cells, 267 libraries), with effects computed as for the essential-gene screens. Zero-shot deletion effects were also re-read without rerunning any model, as the effect per control cell expressing the target and as additional features of the same ridge model. Gene regulatory networks 

For each FM, gene-gene networks were derived from contextual gene-embedding similarity and from attention averaged over layers and heads, and compared with CellOracle, Pearson co-expression, an expression prior and random ranking. The top-50 neighbours of each hub gene were scored against knockdown targets (Adamson) and ChIP-seq targets (ChIP-Atlas [34]) in K562, genes responding to EGFR inhibition in treatment-naive cancer cells of the lung-cancer biopsies, as fold enrichment over an expression-matched expectation (FE@50, 1 = chance), pooled over hubs. The same networks were derived from fine-tuned backbones, including each FM adapted to K562 with the hub perturbations held out. 

Fine-tuning 

scGPT was fine-tuned end-to-end for perturbation on Norman or Adamson. Each FM was fine-tuned as a cell-type classifier on every annotation reference, including the organoid screen, with a linear probe, partial unfreezing and LoRA (scGPT also with the full fine-tuning of its tutorial), on 4,000 training and 1,000 validation cells, with the configuration selected on validation and tested once, against logistic regression on principal components (PCA-LR) or on z-scored variable genes, PCA-kNN and CellTypist tuned on the same cells. Knockouts were recomputed with each fine-tuned backbone, and tensor-wise weight checks confirmed which parameters changed. Networks were also derived from each FM adapted to K562 with the hub perturbations held out. Full settings are in the Supplementary Methods. 

Expression-matched specificity test 

To test whether a knockout carries information about the deleted gene beyond the loss of expressed input, the embedding shift after deleting target g in a cell was compared with the shifts after deleting, one at a time, 20 control genes matched to g in that cell for expression and across the dataset for detection rate. The specific effect is S = observed − mean(control) and z = S / sd(control). A gene×cell-type pair with z > 2 has a specific excess shift. In the organoid screen the test was run on the 991 control cells for every zero-shot and fine-tuned backbone (Supplementary Table S6). 

Statistics and computation 

Correlations are Spearman's ρ with two-sided p-values unless stated otherwise. Multiple testing was controlled with the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure [35] within pre-specified families (K562, organoid screen annotation, organoid screen knockouts, mouse cortex, T cells, cell-line screens), and the number of tests is reported in the Supplementary Tables (for the organoid screen in Supplementary Table S7). The analyses of the organoid screen, the mouse cortex and the genome-scale screens followed an analysis plan written before their results (available in the repository), and every analysis run is reported. All computation ran on SLURM nodes (NVIDIA A100 and L40 GPUs) at CNR-ITB.

Annotation: foundation models help mainly across species 

On primary immune cells, zero-shot kNN transfer from FM embeddings reached accuracies of 0.887 (Geneformer), 0.912 (scGPT), 0.926 (Nicheformer) and 0.949 (scFoundation). PCA with the same classifier reached 0.945 and CellTypist 0.921 (Figure 2A, Supplementary Table S8). On Tabula Muris lung, PCA (0.945, macro-F1 0.844) exceeded all FMs (0.839 to 0.902, macro-F1 0.635 to 0.765, Figure 2C, Supplementary Figure S1, Supplementary Table S9). Mapping the lung-cancer biopsies onto the HLCA, FMs (0.91 to 0.94, except Geneformer V1 at 0.605) were close to PCA (0.924) and CellTypist (0.913, Supplementary Table S10). From human to mouse (Supplementary Figure S2, Supplementary Tables S11 to S15), Nicheformer (0.968), Geneformer V2 (0.914) and scFoundation (0.898) exceeded CellTypist with default settings (0.684) and cosine-kNN baselines (0.863), but CellTypist with tuned regularisation reached 0.941 (macro-F1 0.883), so only Nicheformer remained above every baseline. From mouse to human, no FM was separable from cosine-kNN PCA (0.945). Only Nicheformer was pre-trained on mouse cells (Supplementary Table S16). Fine-tuned classifiers raised macro-F1 over zero-shot kNN within Tabula Muris lung for all five models (by 0.09 to 0.20), but only Geneformer V2 exceeded the best baseline trained on the same cells (0.892 versus 0.868). Across species fine-tuning lowered human-to-mouse macro-F1 below zero-shot for every model, whereas Geneformer V2 improved mouse-to-human transfer from 0.789 to 0.879, above every same-split baseline (at most 0.736, Supplementary Figure S3, Supplementary Table S17). With the full reference, zero-shot Nicheformer reached 0.894 and cosine-kNN PCA 0.862 (Supplementary Table S3). 

Annotation of organoid screen cells: principal components remain the reference 

On the 28,344 cells of the CHOOSE organoid screen (16 cell types), no FM matched PCA in zero-shot annotation within the dataset: PCA with kNN reached accuracy 0.906 and macro-F1 0.892, CellTypist 0.852 and 0.812, and the best FM, Geneformer V2, 0.846 and 0.778, followed by scFoundation (0.796 and 0.700) and Nicheformer (0.760 and 0.620, Figure 2, Supplementary Table S18). Among the scGPT checkpoints the brain checkpoint was the best (macro-F1 0.594, against 0.550 for the original and 0.515 for the continual-pretrained whole-human checkpoint), about 0.30 below PCA. Mapped onto HNOCA and scored as broad-lineage concordance, all embeddings reached accuracies of 0.605 to 0.698. scFoundation was the most accurate (0.698 against 0.658 for PCA) with a macro-F1 equal to that of PCA (difference +0.002, 95% CI −0.004 to 0.008), and the ranking changed when the HNOCA label Glioblast was accepted for radial glia (Geneformer V2 0.850, PCA 0.731). Fine-tuning on 4,000 cells improved every model over its own zero-shot embedding (macro-F1 gain 0.046 to 0.310), but for all five models the arm selected on validation stayed below PCA-kNN trained on the same cells (macro-F1 0.885, differences −0.041 to −0.083, all q < 0.05, Supplementary Figure S3, Supplementary Table S19). 

Perturbation in cell lines: zero-shot deletions do not predict effect size 

For every representable single-gene perturbation of Norman and Adamson (75 to 152 per dataset), the predicted deletion magnitude was unrelated to the real effect size: Spearman's ρ between predicted cosine shift and the number of real DEGs ranged from −0.08 to 0.06 for the four FMs, none significant, and ranking perturbations by target expression did no better (ρ = −0.03 and 0.06, Figure 3A). The direction of the shift was more informative. A deletion should move cells away from a CRISPRa response: Geneformer V2 and scFoundation did so for 76% and 75% of Norman perturbations (both p < 1e-8). For Adamson knockdowns scGPT (70%, p = 0.0009) and Nicheformer (71%, p = 0.0002) shifted cells in the real direction (Figure 3B, Supplementary Tables S20 to S25). With Geneformer's own protocol, which models CRISPRa as overexpression, Geneformer V2 predicted Norman effect sizes (ρ = 0.30, p = 0.0002, BH q = 0.001) whereas V1 did not (ρ = −0.04, Figure 3A, Supplementary Table S26).

Perturbation in neural tissue: predicted effects track target expression 

In the 991 control cells of the organoid screen, the predicted effect of deleting each of the 36 genes followed the target's expression in each cell type for every zero-shot model (ρ = 0.56 to 0.93 over 504 gene by cell-type pairs, highest for Nicheformer and Geneformer V1, Figure 4A, Supplementary Tables S33 to S35), and the same held in the mouse cortex (ρ = 0.70 to 0.97 over gene by class pairs, Supplementary Table S36). Fine-tuning as a cell-type classifier rescaled these effects (median ratio to zero-shot 0.85 to 6.24) while preserving their ranking (ρ = 0.77 to 0.99 over pairs, Figure 4B, Supplementary Table S37). 

Measured effects in neural tissue: no model exceeds naive predictors 

In the organoid screen none of the 36 tests of the predefined rule was met (18 zero-shot and fine-tuned predictors by two ground truths, Figure 4C, Supplementary Table S38). Five predictions were positive at q < 0.05, Geneformer V2 against the number of DEGs (ρ = 0.156) and Geneformer V1, Nicheformer, Nicheformer with partial unfreezing and scFoundation with LoRA against composition changes (ρ = 0.133 to 0.181), but none exceeded the best naive predictor by 0.10: the number of control cells per cell type reached ρ = 0.206 on the DEG truth and the detection rate of the target 0.130 on the composition truth. The DEG associations did not exceed a null that permutes genes within cell type, so they reflect differences between cell types, and the composition associations did not exceed a null that permutes genes within expression decile, so they reflect target expression, as does the detection rate. A larger model version improves annotation but not perturbation 

We repeated every Geneformer row with the V2-316M release (Supplementary Table S22). V2 annotated better than V1 on immune cells, Tabula Muris lung, the biopsies, the organoid screen (macro-F1 0.778 versus 0.504) and from human to mouse (0.914 versus 0.642). Zero-shot deletion results did not change: V2 tracked neither the organoid screen nor the mouse cortex effects beyond naive predictors, and its network stayed near chance against K562 knockdown targets (FE@50 1.02). Scale therefore helped annotation and, with the overexpression protocol, CRISPRa effect sizes. 

Knockout effects are mostly non-specific 

The expression-matched specificity test explains why predicted effects follow target expression (Figure 5, Supplementary Table S6). In the organoid screen, deleting the target shifted the embedding no more than deleting one of 20 control genes matched for expression in the same cell: the target exceeded all 20 controls in 1.1% to 5.7% of gene by cell-type pairs, against 4.8% expected by chance (binomial p ≥ 0.19), and the fraction of pairs with z > 2 was 0.9% to 6.6%. The median ratio of target to matched-control effect was close to 1 for the rank-encoded models (Geneformer V1 and V2 0.98, Nicheformer 0.99) and below 1 for the value-encoded models (scGPT 0.49 to 0.56, scFoundation 0.28). Once the matched null was subtracted, the correlation of the effect with target expression disappeared or became negative (ρ −0.73 to −0.04). Removing the non-specific component did not improve agreement with real knockouts. In K562, the specific effect correlated with the number of DEGs or the energy distance at ρ −0.16 to 0.17 (all p ≥ 0.13, Supplementary Table S45). In the organoid screen only the specific effect of Nicheformer correlated positively with composition changes (ρ = 0.10, p = 0.027), below the detection rate of the target, and no specific effect correlated with DEG numbers.

For K562 ChIP-seq targets the best FM network (scGPT attention, 2.20) was also below co-expression (2.87). Gene representations of FMs predict which knockdowns have large effects 

Because deleting a gene from the input did not reveal how strongly its knockdown changes the cell, we asked whether the gene representations that FMs learn during pretraining carry this information. Two re-readings of the stored deletion effects did not help. Dividing the effect by the fraction of control cells that express the target, which removes its dependence on detection, lowered the correlation in T cells (ρ = −0.03 to 0.06) and met the rule in 0 of 15 T cell and 0 of 20 cell-line tests, and adding the deletion effect to expression, detection and number of perturbed cells improved the out-of-fold prediction in none of 35 tests (Supplementary Tables S82 and S83). Simulating a partial knockdown matched to the residual expression measured in each screen, instead of a deletion, did not change this: it met the rule in 0 of 15 T cell and 0 of 15 cell-line tests. Added to the naive predictors, it gave small gains in 2 of 15 cell-line tests (Geneformer V2 in K562, 0.048, q = 0.037, and scGPT in neuron CRISPRi, 0.054, q = 0.0498), which disappeared with a fixed residual expression of 0.2. Compared with deletion on the same targets, knockdown did not change ρ in T cells, lowered it for scGPT and scFoundation in K562 (by 0.12 for both) and raised it by 0.01 for Geneformer V1 in K562 and RPE1 (Supplementary Methods). Gene vectors trained on one system did not transfer to another: from K562 to RPE1, from RPE1 to K562 and from either to T cells no FM exceeded expression or STRING degree (0 of 20 tests, for example ρ = 0.20 for scGPT against 0.37 for STRING degree from RPE1 to K562, Supplementary Table S84). Within a system, however, adding the gene vectors of scGPT or Nicheformer to expression features improved the prediction of the number of DEGs in K562 (ρ from 0.39 to 0.46 and from 0.40 to 0.46) and in T cells (from 0.20 to 0.25 and to 0.24), above 200 residual-permutation nulls (p = 0.005, the smallest attainable, Supplementary Figure S9, Supplementary Tables S85 and S86). In the genome-wide CRISPRi screen of K562 [13], with 7,509 and 7,557 targets never used in this study, the gene vectors of scGPT and Nicheformer raised the correlation from 0.26 to 0.43 and 0.41 (gain 0.17 and 0.15, 95% intervals 0.15 to 0.19 and 0.13 to 0.17, p = 0.005, q = 0.005), whereas the 95th percentile of the gains on 100 fully random truths was 0.05. ## Discussion 

Evaluated against simple baselines on the same data, current single-cell FMs helped in specific settings: mapping a reference across species (also for newer models such as UCE), the direction of perturbation responses in K562 (only faintly in genome-scale screens), CRISPRa effect sizes with Geneformer's own overexpression protocol and, with patient-level ground truth, tumour regulatory networks. Within a species their embeddings support label transfer about as well as principal components, as reported for zero-shot clustering [5,6], although fine-tuned Geneformer V2 exceeded them on Tabula Muris lung, and on organoid screen cells principal components stayed ahead also after fine-tuning. Across species the margin over CellTypist depended on tuning its regularisation, which a fair benchmark must do. Their zero-shot in silico knockouts do not predict how strongly a real knockout perturbs a cell line and, in neural tissue, largely restate where the target gene is expressed. Their gene-gene networks do not recover knockdown targets or ChIP-seq binding better than co-expression. These results extend earlier conclusions from cell lines [5,6,7,8] to genome-scale screens in primary T cells and two cell lines, an organoid screen, an in vivo screen and tumour biopsies. Perturbation screens in tissue provide a measured effect for every gene and cell type, but in both tissue screens analysed here the measured effect replicated only partly between libraries, and naive predictors that know nothing about gene function, the number of perturbed or control cells and the detection rate of the target, reached the correlations of the best models. In the K562 and RPE1 screens, by contrast, the measured effects and their difference between the two cell lines replicated almost perfectly between libraries, and no model predicted them either, so a noisy ground truth does not explain the failure. Associations found with per-gene statistics disappeared under a null that permutes genes within cell type, which should therefore accompany any such score. Agreement between methods was here mostly shared dependence on target expression, which a naive predictor reproduces. The specificity test gives a mechanism for this dependence and a practical control. Deleting a gene from a cell's input changes the embedding roughly as much as deleting any other gene of similar expression, so the per-cell-type effect mostly measures how many cells express the target. In the organoid screen the target exceeded its matched controls no more often than expected by chance, and the excess did not predict measured effects. We propose that in silico perturbation results be reported against such a null. The test is implemented in fmaudit. Fine-tuning did not change this picture. It improved classification over each model's own embedding without reaching principal components within a species, rescaled predicted effects up to six-fold without changing their ranking, and did not yield better gene networks, so it altered sensitivity rather than correctness. Supervised models trained on Perturb-seq of cell lines and primary T cells or on the organoid screen itself, including GEARS with scFoundation embeddings, did not beat the mean training shift for unseen genes and cannot be applied to systems without perturbation data. Without the checks applied here, a fine-tuning run that silently fell back to the pretrained model would have suggested that fine-tuning leaves predictions unchanged. An input re-sampled before and after each deletion would have made scGPT's effects unrelated to the deleted gene. A cell-level ground truth would have rewarded predictors of cell number, and a per-gene statistic without a within-cell-type null would have rewarded differences between cell types. Running Nicheformer without its species and assay tokens changed its values, although not the conclusions (Supplementary Table S91). Table 1 summarises the resulting recommendations, including atarget-detection check now in fmaudit. Limitations: the mouse cortex screen has only 374 control cells and measured effects that replicate poorly between libraries, and the organoid screen has a modest replication ceiling, so both test the models against a noisy truth. Three mouse targets had no human ortholog in our table. In silico deletion represents only cell-autonomous effects, and FMs see only expression, so missense variants will require models that combine expression with sequence. When Recommendation Annotating cells within one species Start with label transfer on principal components or with CellTypist. In this benchmark zero-shot FM embeddings did not do better. ## Conclusions 

Across three tasks, from cell lines and primary T cells to an organoid screen, an in vivo screen and tumour biopsies, single-cell FMs were matched by simple baselines in most comparisons, and helped mainly to transfer labels across species and, in K562, to give the direction of perturbation responses. No model predicted measured knockout effects beyond naive predictors and permutation nulls, in neural tissue or in genome-scale screens whose effects replicate. The gene vectors learned in pretraining by seven of the eight models tested, in contrast, predicted which knockdowns have large effects beyond expression, confirmed on an independent screen, which suggests using FMs as a source of gene features rather than as simulators of deletions. Task-matched baselines, these nulls, replication ceilings and pipeline checks should become a standard component of FM benchmarks.

The first level covers cell lines and primary cells: K562 Norman CRISPRa, Adamson CRISPRi, K562 and RPE1 essential-gene CRISPRi and the primary CD4 T cell CRISPRi screen. The second covers neural tissue: the CHOOSE organoid screen, the in vivo mouse cortex screen and the iPSC-derived neuron CRISPRi and CRISPRa screens. The third covers human tissue (NSCLC biopsies). (B) Scorecard of all task by dataset rows. Colour shows each method's value minus the best non-FM baseline of the row, clipped at plus or minus 0.3, and text shows the value. Rows use different metrics, so colours compare methods within a row only. For the organoid screen and mouse cortex knockouts, values are pooled Spearman ρ over gene by cell-type (or class) pairs of the zero-shot predictions. The truths are the measured number of DEGs, the composition change (organoid screen) and the summed |log2FC| (mouse cortex, GFP reference). The baseline is the best naive predictor on the same pairs. The fine-tuned column of these rows gives the best of the eleven fine-tuned backbones. The CellOracle values of the organoid screen cover the six genes it can simulate and are coloured against the best naive predictor on the same pairs. For the primary CD4 T cell, K562 and RPE1 essential-gene and iPSC neuron CRISPRi and CRISPRa screens, values are Spearman ρ between the zero-shot deletion effect and the real number of DE genes over 600, 600, 600, 184 and 100 targets (T cells, resting cells against the resting truth). The baseline is the best naive predictor on all targets, and each model is coloured against the best naive predictor recomputed on its own targets. For the scPerturb screens the best naive predictor is chosen, and subtracted, by its absolute ρ, as pre-registered. The gene-vector rows report ridge regression on FM gene vectors, scored as pooled out-of-fold Spearman ρ with p and q from 200 Freedman-Lane null truths. In K562 genome-wide, the knockdown effect size predicted from gene vectors plus expression features reached ρ 0.43 for scGPT, 0.41 for Nicheformer, 0.37 for Geneformer V2 and 0.34 for Geneformer V1 (q at most 0.0075) and 0.25 for scFoundation (q 0.12, not confirmed), against 0.26 for the expression features alone (0.258 to 0.261 across the analyses). Each model is coloured against its own expression-only baseline (Supplementary Figure S9). In the transfer between systems, no FM exceeded the baseline in any of the 20 tests. The best FM test is shown (scGPT, RPE1 to K562, ρ 0.20), against the best baseline, STRING degree (ρ 0.37). In the supervised K562 Norman and Adamson rows the scFoundation column is scFoundation with GEARS (¶ 99 of 106 Norman and 20 of 22 Adamson test perturbations, against the training mean on the same perturbations). In the supervised rows of the genome-scale screens the scGPT column is scGPT with GEARS, the best supervised column is GEARS and the baseline is the training mean, with the split-half ceiling in the sub-label (Pearson δ on the top 20 DE genes, five-fold target hold-out over 600 targets for T cells, GEARS split with 103 and 163 test perturbations for K562 and RPE1). The T cell GRN row gives the pooled expression-matched FE@50 of up to 75 transcription-factor hubs against the authors' DE genes. Each FM value is the best of its embedding and attention networks (named in the sub-label) and is coloured against signed Pearson co-expression on the same hubs. The Geneformer V2 values of the K562 Norman and neuron CRISPRa rows use Geneformer's overexpression protocol. In the annotation rows the fine-tuned column gives the best test accuracy over the five FMs fine-tuned as classifiers, each arm (linear probe, partial unfreezing or LoRA) chosen on validation on a 5,000-cell subsample of each reference, against the best baseline trained on the same cells. The two cross-species rows use CellTypist tuned on the same cells as baseline. The organoid screen fine-tuned row shows each model's arm selected on validation against PCA-kNN trained on the same cells. PD ctrl is the same score against the progression control. ‡ STATE evaluated on perturbations seen in training in other cell lines. In training data means that STATE was not scored because the Replogle essential-gene screens are in its training data. Unsupp. means target not in the model vocabulary (supported/total targets), n/a not evaluated or not run, not assessed an FM not included in the gene-vector analysis, not shown an FM transfer test below the best one shown, and NA not applicable. Geneformer V1 and V2 are shown as separate columns.

Figure 8. Genome-scale perturbation screens. (A) Spearman ρ (95% bootstrap CI) between the zero-shot deletion effect and the real number of DE genes, per model and screen. For primary CD4 T cells, the predictions come from 200 resting non-targeting control cells and are scored against the authors' DE genes in resting cells and, with the same predictions, against the 8 h and 48 h stimulation truths (593 to 600 targets). The other screens are K562 and RPE1 essential-gene CRISPRi (the same 600 targets in both), iPSC-derived neuron CRISPRi (176 to 180 targets) and CRISPRa (96 or 97 targets, plus Geneformer overexpression, diamonds). Filled markers are BH q < 0.05 within the pre-registered families (15 T cell tests, 20 scPerturb tests, 4 overexpression tests). Crosses are the permutation null with real effects permuted within expression deciles (97.5% quantile for T cells, 95% quantile for the scPerturb screens, as computed by each track). Dashed lines are the best naive predictor on all targets (value and predictor in the group header). Red dotted lines are the replication ceiling. For T cells this is the mean donor-versus-donor ρ of the per-donor edgeR recomputation (6 donor pairs). For the scPerturb screens it is library half A against half B. (B) ρ minus the best naive predictor on the same targets, with the paired bootstrap 95% CI. For the scPerturb screens the absolute ρ of the best naive predictor is subtracted. The dotted line marks the margin of 0.10 required by the decision rule. No model meets the rule in any test (0 of 15 T cell tests, 0 of 20 scPerturb tests). (C) Supervised models. Points are the Pearson δ on the top 20 DE genes of each held-out target (T cells, five-fold target hold-out over 600 targets in resting cells) or test perturbation (K562 and RPE1, GEARS simulation split, 103 and 163 test perturbations). They are shown for GEARS, scGPT with GEARS, the training-mean prediction and the split-half ceiling. Diamonds are means. The labels give the mean and the mean difference from the training mean with its 95% CI. The solid grey line is the training mean and the red dotted line the split-half ceiling. (D) T cell networks. Pooled expression-matched fold enrichment of the top 50 predicted targets (FE@50) of transcription-factor hubs, for embedding (circles) and attention (squares) networks extracted from 2,000 resting non-targeting cells. Filled markers are pooled p < 0.05. The dashed line is signed Pearson co-expression on the same cells and the dash-dot line the expression prior. Absolute Spearman co-expression is shown as a sensitivity reference. Left: authors' DE genes at 10% FDR (75 hubs, 71 to 75 per model). Right: DE genes replicated in at least 3 donors (39 hubs).
