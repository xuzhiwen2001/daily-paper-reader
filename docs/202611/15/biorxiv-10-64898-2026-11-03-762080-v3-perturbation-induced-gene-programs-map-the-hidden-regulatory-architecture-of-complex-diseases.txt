Abstract 32 

Steady-state molecular profiles incompletely capture the regulatory programs through which genetic 33 

variation influences complex disease, particularly programs that emerge only when cells are perturbed. 34 

Here, we introduce Perturbscape, a computational framework that learns multiscale gene programs for 35 

individual perturbations in a CRISPR Perturb-seq screen using linear and nonlinear contrastive 36 

approaches alongside complementary program-learning methods, and integrates them with human 37 

genetics to prioritize disease-relevant genes, perturbations and downstream regulatory pathways. 38

and traits (mean GWAS N=291K), perturbation-induced programs showed up to 4.3-fold higher 40 

heritability informativeness than analogous steady-state multi-tissue gene prioritization, and retained 41

Introduction 58 

Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have linked tens of thousands of loci to complex human 59 

diseases and traits, but identifying the causal genes and regulatory programs that mediate this genetic 60 

risk remains a central bottleneck in human genetics 1–4 . Steady-state molecular annotations do not 61 

effectively capture regulatory programs that emerge only following a gene perturbation or within specific 62 

cellular, developmental or activation contexts 5,6 . CRISPR Perturb-seq screens now measure, directly 63 

and at scale, the downstream transcriptional consequences of experimentally perturbing individual 64 

genes 7–9 , revealing these context-specific trans-regulatory programs. Recent studies have begun to 65 

connect such programs to disease, relating the trans-regulatory targets of GWAS-implicated genes to 66 

specific disease pathways 10,11 and prioritizing perturbed genes through the heritability enrichment of 67 

their downstream differentially expressed genes 12–14 . Recent work has further integrated Perturb-seq 68 

regulatory effects with gene-level loss-of-function burden associations to construct regulator–program– 69 

trait models for blood traits, illustrating the potential of perturbation-derived regulatory structure for 70 

interpreting human genetic effects 15 . However, existing approaches have generally examined a limited 71 

range of transcriptional programs and cellular systems or relied on well-powered quantitative rare-72 

variant associations for regulatory modeling. 73 

For a disease-relevant perturbation, we expect its trans -regulatory gene programs to preferentially 74 

contain genes enriched for cis -genetic disease signal at their linked loci 13,15 . Building on this principle, 75 

we present Perturbscape, an end-to-end computational framework that operationalizes this cis–trans 76 

convergence to connect a perturbation from a CRISPR Perturb-seq screen to the genetic architecture 77 

of a complex disease. From a single screen, Perturbscape constructs a multiscale atlas of gene 78 

programs for each perturbation using several linear and nonlinear contrastive learning approaches 79 

alongside complementary canonical and local gene-program learners. These programs capture global 80 

and locally coordinated transcriptional responses, including regulatory variation selectively enriched 81 

following perturbation relative to control cells. Perturbscape integrates these programs into optimized 82 

disease-specific weighted representations, termed meta-programs (denoted MetaProg (𝑝) for 83 

perturbation 𝑝 ), and examines the relevance of these meta-programs to disease heritability 16–18 .84 

Complementary to approaches that prioritize genes using precompiled steady-state expression, 85 

pathway or protein network annotations 19,20 , Perturbscape derives its features directly from perturbation 86 

atlases that sample experimentally induced regulatory states and identifies those most relevant to 87 

matched disease genetics. 88

cell differentiation time course and an in vivo brain screen, and evaluated against 53 matched diseases 95 

and quantitative traits (mean GWAS 𝑁 = 290,733). Across these systems, atlas-wide perturbation 96

Together, these analyses establish perturbation-induced gene programs as a complementary 107 

representation of complex disease regulatory architecture beyond steady-state molecular atlases. 108

Overview of the Perturbscape framework 111 

The central premise of Perturbscape is that disease-relevant perturbations preferentially induce trans-112 

regulatory responses in which the downstream genes are enriched for cis-genetic disease signal at 113 

their linked loci 12,13 (Figure 1a ). Perturbscape first constructs a multi-resolution library of gene 114 

programs corresponding to each perturbation in the screen, rather than relying on a single differential-115 

expression signature or latent representation ( Figure 1b ). For each perturbation, we derive candidate 116 

programs using complementary differential expression and co-expression methods (DE, DGCA 21 ), 117 

canonical latent factor methods (PCA, cNMF 22 ), contrastive methods (contrastive PCA, kernel 118 

contrastive PCA, and ContrastiveVI 23,24 ), and a graph-based gene-module detection method (Hotspot 25 )119 

(Figure 1b ; Methods ; Table S1 ). A distinctive component of this library is a family of contrastive PC 120 

learners that includes the ContraPC and kernel ContraPC formulations introduced here, designed to 121 

isolate transcriptional variation selectively enriched by a focal perturbation relative to control cells 122

Perturbscape prioritizes a perturbation based on the heritability informativeness of its meta-program. 161 

For each perturbation, we link the top 500 MetaProg (𝑝 ) genes to variants using tissue-matched 162 

ENCODE-rE2G enhancer–gene predictions 29 , and quantify the heritability contribution of the resulting 163

Relevance Score, TRS (𝑝 ) , with significance determined by a block jackknife approach ( Methods ,166

value. We use 𝜏 ∗ as the primary metric because (i) unlike enrichment, it is less sensitive to differences 168 

in annotation size, (ii) it quantifies the unique heritability informativeness conditional on the baseline 169

level disease prioritization learned from the complete perturbation atlas and is analogous to PoPS 19 ,171 

but derives its features from experimentally induced perturbation programs within an assayed system 172 

rather than from precompiled multi-tissue and functional annotations. Evaluating both MetaProg (ALL) 173 

and PoPS top N genes using consistently constructed variant annotations and the same conditional S-174

respective gene-prioritization strategies. The top 500 per-perturbation MetaProg (𝑝 ) genes are used for 176 

pathway enrichment analysis and constructing perturbation-pathway-disease triplets ( Figure 1e ). To 177 

visualize the disease-relevant perturbation landscape, we embed perturbation-specific and all-178

Perturbscape resolves gene- and perturbation-level red blood cell trait genetics in genome-wide 183 

K562 Perturb-seq 184 

We applied Perturbscape to a genome-wide CRISPRi Perturb-seq screen in K562, an erythroleukemia 185 

cell line that captures aspects of hematopoietic progenitor and erythroid biology 9 (7,991 perturbations 186 

with ≥100 cells each, 1.8 million cells). We focused on two well-powered hematologic biomarker GWAS 187 

traits whose genetic architectures have previously shown strong relevance to K562 15,31 : mean 188 

corpuscular hemoglobin (MCH) and red blood cell count (RBC count) 32 (average GWAS N=444,430). 189 

Figure 2a presents the UMAP embedding of the per-perturbation meta-programs for these two red 190 

blood cell traits, colored by the TRS scores. Higher TRS scores localized to discrete regions of the 191 

embedding exhibiting specific pathway enrichments among their corresponding meta-programs 192 

(Figure S2a ). Relative to the individual-perturbation TRS scores, the atlas meta-program MetaProg (ALL) 193 

showed at least 2.7x and 2.3x higher TRS for MCH and RBC, respectively ( Figure 2b , S2b ), and its 194 

annotations explained 2.0x and 1.8x greater per-SNP heritability enrichment than the strongest 195 

individual perturbations. This atlas-level signal reflected distributed contributions across perturbations 196 

rather than dominance by a small number of highly redundant programs: for MCH and RBC respectively, 197 

68% and 77% of perturbations shared fewer than 10% of their top 500 MetaProg (𝑝 ) genes with the top 198 

500 MetaProg (ALL) genes ( Figure S2c ). For both traits, MetaProg (ALL) was enriched in canonical 199 

erythropoietic pathways such as RUNX1 transcriptional regulation and Kit receptor signaling ( Figure 200 

S2d ). Joint heritability analysis of the meta-programs retained MetaProg (ALL) together with 2 nominally 201 

significant (P < 0.05) perturbation-specific annotations for each of MCH and RBC: SIGLEC14 and 202 

UBE2V1 for MCH, and RCL1 and MYL6B for RBC ( Methods , Figure 2b , S2b ). SIGLEC14 and MYL6B 203 

implicated distinct pathways, including CREB1 phosphorylation (p adj = 7.7e-02) and G  signaling (p adj 204 

= 1e-02), respectively ( Table 2 ). 205 

We next benchmarked the disease-gene prioritization performance of Perturbscape against PoPS 206 

using rare-variant gene burden associations 33,34 across 7 functional variant masks with alternate allele 207 

frequency (AAF) < 0.001 ( Methods ). For matched comparison with Perturbscape, we re-fit PoPS using 208 

its original feature set but restricted to the universe of expressed genes in the perturbation data. For 209 

MCH, the top 500 MetaProg (ALL) genes exhibited 1.8-fold enrichment (P=1.2e-04) among burden genes 210 

implicated by the aggregate synonymous + missense + loss-of-function (LoF) mask, whereas top 500 211 

PoPS genes showed no significant enrichment (1.2x enrichment, P=0.18) ( Figure 2c ). Alignment was 212 

strongest for missense-mask burden and weaker for LoF and synonymous masks. Because the 213 

common-variant MAGMA and rare-variant burden gene sets showed minimal direct overlap (Jaccard 214 

index < 0.063 across masks) ( Figure S2e ), these results indicate that Perturbscape meta-programs 215 

capture pathway-level mechanistic convergence between the common and rare genetic architectures 216 

of red blood cell traits. Notably, extending prior observations of common–rare pathway convergence 35– 217 37 , perturbation-derived gene programs captured this convergence more strongly than the steady-state 218 

features in PoPS, demonstrating that, in this setting, experimentally induced gene programs can 219 

capture common–rare genetic convergence not recovered as strongly by steady-state PoPS features. 220 

By contrast, genes ranked directly by perturbation TRS were not enriched for burden genes (P = 0.56– 221 

0.94 across masks), indicating that an upstream perturbed gene need not show direct genetic 222 

association for its downstream program to be disease relevant, as also noted in refs 12–14 . Perturbation 223 

TRS was not correlated with the MAGMA z-scores of the perturbed genes ( Figure S2f ), further 224 

suggesting that upstream perturbed genes driving disease-relevant programs need not themselves be 225 

GWAS-implicated genes. 226

Both TRS (ALL) and the corresponding heritability enrichment increased markedly with the number of 229 

perturbations for MCH and RBC count ( Figure 2d , S2g -h). Statistical significance also generally 230 

increased with the number of perturbations, but with a weaker trend ( Figure S2h ). Importantly, this 231

RBC count, respectively), although somewhat less strongly than MetaProg (ALL) and PoPS. This 254 

demonstrates the convergence between cis-regulatory evidence from combining CRISPR and GWAS 255 

with the trans pathway level convergence from Perturbscape. 256

Discussion 561 

Perturbscape provides a general framework for interpreting human disease genetics through 562 

transcriptional programs revealed by experimental perturbations. Rather than representing each 563 

perturbation by a single differential-expression signature or a small number of shared programs, 564 

Perturbscape captures multi-scale regulatory programs within each perturbation and uses human 565 

genetics to identify the combinations most relevant to disease. This formulation produces two 566 

complementary views of disease biology: MetaProg (ALL) integrates disease-relevant gene programs 567 

distributed across an entire perturbation atlas to prioritize disease genes, whereas perturbation-specific 568 

meta-programs can assess the extent to which the downstream consequences of a single perturbation 569 

are enriched for disease heritability. Perturbscape complements recent work by Ota et al. 15 , which 570 

integrates quantitative rare-variant loss-of-function effects with Perturb-seq regulatory measurements 571 

to infer directional regulator–program–trait relationships for hematological traits. While the latter 572

It is made The copyright holder for this preprint this version posted November 11, 2026. ;https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.03.762080 doi: bioRxiv preprint

approach enables directional mechanistic inference, its reliance on sufficiently powered gene-level 573 

loss-of-function associations may limit its applicability across complex diseases and traits for which 574 

such associations remain sparse. In contrast, Perturbscape uses common-variant GWAS to 575 

systematically identify disease-relevant perturbations and downstream regulatory programs across 576 

diverse cellular contexts, without incorporating rare-variant associations into program learning or 577 

disease-conditioned meta-program construction. Instead, rare-variant burden associations provide an 578 

independent source of genetic validation, demonstrating convergence of common- and rare-variant 579 

disease signals within perturbation-induced regulatory programs. Across matched diseases and traits, 580 

MetaProg (ALL) provided competitive or improved disease-gene prioritization relative to the integrative 581 

multi-tissue PoPS framework 19 while prioritizing complementary disease genes. This demonstrates 582 

that perturbation atlases provide a complementary form of regulatory diversity to steady-state multi-583 

tissue resources by sampling experimentally induced states within disease-relevant cellular systems. 584 

Concordance with common- and rare-variant associations, enhancer CRISPRi screens, pharmacologic 585 

responses and population trans-eQTLs further supports the utility of these perturbation-derived 586 

programs for connecting genetic variation to context-specific cellular biology. 587 

Perturbscape has several downstream applications. First, the perturbation–pathway–disease maps can 588 

prioritize perturbations and downstream pathways for therapeutic investigation, as illustrated by the 589 

statin-associated programs in HepG2. Second, the multiscale perturbation-induced program atlas 590 

provides a structured representation of cellular responses to genetic interventions that could inform the 591 

development of virtual-cell 77–79 and cellular world models 80,81 . In particular, perturbation-derived 592 

programs could serve as interpretable response representations for modeling cellular state transitions 593 

and predicting responses to unseen perturbations, while disease-conditioned meta-programs could 594 

provide genetically anchored objectives or evaluation criteria for assessing whether predicted cellular 595 

responses capture disease-relevant regulatory biology. Third, disease-relevant perturbations and 596 

genes within their meta-programs could guide subsequent experimental rounds, enabling iterative 597 

disease-genetics-guided map–perturb–predict 82 or lab-in-the-loop 83 active-learning frameworks. 598 

Finally, integrating perturbation-derived programs across multiple Perturb-seq datasets with 599 

complementary steady-state and functional annotations could further improve disease-gene 600 

prioritization, extending the previous works 19,84 .601 

The Perturbscape framework has several limitations. First, it is currently formulated primarily for single-602 

perturbation designs, including low-MOI pooled screens and clonally engineered perturbations. In high-603 

MOI screens, co-occurring perturbations complicate attribution of cellular responses to individual 604 

targets; extending Perturbscape to this setting will require counterfactual-guided programs that isolate 605 

each perturbation while accounting for co-perturbation composition. Second, Perturbscape’s ability to 606 

recover a perturbation-induced gene program depends on perturbation coverage, perturbation 607 

efficiency, the number of profiled cells and the cellular states represented in a screen. Third, the current 608 

framework focuses primarily on transcriptional consequences of single-gene perturbations at discrete 609 

endpoints and does not explicitly model time-resolved perturbation dynamics 85 , combinatorial genetic 610 

interactions 86 , or perturbations with multi-omic readouts 87,88 . Fourth, enrichment of a perturbation-611 

specific meta-program for disease heritability establishes genetic relevance but should not be 612 

interpreted as causal. It does not by itself identify the causal variant or establish that the perturbed gene 613 

mediates the association; this is characteristic of most scalable program-to-disease mapping 614 

approaches in the literature 10,13,16,19 . Fifth, like PoPS, Perturbscape relies on sufficiently polygenic 615 

genome-wide association signal to learn stable relationships between gene programs and disease 616 

genetics and may therefore have reduced power for traits with sparse genetic architectures or 617 

underpowered GWAS. Sixth, Perturbscape currently summarizes multiple guides targeting the same 618 

gene into a gene-level perturbation, which increases cell numbers and robustness to guide-specific 619 

technical variation for program learning but can obscure biologically meaningful heterogeneity among 620 

guides. Future extensions that model guides hierarchically within genes could address this limitation. 621

Perturb-seq datasets, preprocessing and QC. 627 

We analyzed seven Perturb-seq systems spanning CRISPRi, CRISPR–Cas9 knockout, stem-cell 628 

differentiation and in vivo brain perturbation experiments ( Table 1 ). We restricted analyses to cells or 629 

nuclei with an unambiguous assignment to a single genetic perturbation, including dual-guide 630 

constructs targeting the same gene where applicable, and excluded cells with assignments to multiple 631 

distinct target genes. Non-targeting and safe-targeting guides were retained as controls. Perturbations 632 

were analyzed only when represented by at least 100 cells, with a threshold of 200 cells applied to the 633 

K562 essential-gene and Jurkat datasets. No threshold was applied to the pancreatic differentiation 634 

dataset, in which all targeted lines were retained and the number of perturbations reported reflects 635 

those yielding at least one gene program. Guides targeting the same gene were aggregated to define 636 

a gene-level perturbation for the primary analyses; guide-level analyses were performed separately 637 

where indicated. This design therefore focuses on single-perturbation experiments, including low-MOI 638 

pooled CRISPR screens and clonally engineered single-target perturbations. 639 

For all datasets, expression values were normalized to counts per 10,000 per cell and log-transformed 640 

(log CP10k + 1). The single exception was the genome-wide K562 Perturb-seq dataset, for which we 641 

used the z-score-normalized expression representation provided by the original study 9. For the 642 

genome-wide K562 Perturb-seq dataset, control cells greatly outnumbered the cells assigned to any 643 

individual perturbation. We therefore randomly subsampled 10% of control cells, stratified by GEM 644 

group so that each group contributed in proportion to its representation in the full control population. 645 

This subsampled control set was used for all K562 analyses. Raw count matrices were available for 646 

every dataset and were retained alongside the normalized values, providing the input for the models 647 

that operate directly on counts. Gene programs were derived within a dataset-specific gene universe 648 

capped at 10,000 genes; gene sets below this threshold were retained in full, and larger sets were 649 

reduced to the 10,000 most highly variable genes. For the linear contrastive methods, each population 650 

is standardized to zero mean and unit variance per gene. For the kernel methods, each population is 651 

min–max scaled to the unit interval, satisfying the bounded-domain assumption required by random-652 

feature approximations 89,90 . Before transformation, the workflow checks whether the input already 653 

satisfies the required scaling and, if so, passes the values through unchanged to avoid repeated 654 

transformation. 655

Clustering perturbations and characterizing clusters 942 

We partitioned the perturbations into clusters using Leiden clustering. We first constructed a k-nearest-943 

neighbor graph from the binarized meta-programs—binary vectors indicating whether each gene 944 

ranked among the top 500 by each meta-program's scores—using 15 neighbors and the Jaccard index 945 

as the distance metric. We then applied Leiden clustering with the Python package leidenalg (v0.10.2), 946

model was fit using directly genotyped array variants to estimate genome-wide polygenic effects and 965

contamination. In Step 2, rare coding variants from the WES data were aggregated within genes 969

LoF+missense, LoF+synonymous, missense+synonymous, and LoF+missense+synonymous. For 971

(a) Perturbscape prioritizes CRISPR perturbations by mapping gene programs underlying their induced 1450

(b) Construction of a comprehensive perturbation-response program library across diverse 1454 

experimental systems. For each perturbation in a Perturb-seq experiment, complementary 1455 

representation-learning approaches capture distinct aspects of the perturbation response, including 1456 

differential methods (DE/DGCA), canonical dimensionality-reduction methods (cNMF/PCA), 1457

gene programs as features. Programs carrying disease-relevant information are retained and combined 1461 

to estimate an optimally weighted disease meta-program , whereas non-informative programs receive 1462 

little or no contribution. This procedure generates MetaProg (𝑝,𝑑) gene program representations for each 1463 

perturbation–disease pair, as well as a pooled MetaProg (ALL ,𝑑) representation integrating programs 1464 

across all perturbations. 1465

Supplementary Figure 1: The Perturbscape workflow 1752 

Perturb-seq data are analyzed either singly (one contrast per perturbation) or pooled (all perturbations 1753 

against control). Eight modules define gene programs. Contrastive (cPCA, ContraPC and their kernel 1754 

forms) and canonical (PCA, cNMF) methods yield programs directly, as gene loadings or spectra, and 1755 

also pass their cell-level embeddings to Hotspot, which derives further programs from genes co-varying 1756 

across that space; ContrastiveVI contributes through Hotspot alone, DE/DGCA directly only (dashed 1757 

arrow). Per GWAS trait, informative programs are selected by LD-aware regression on gene-level 1758 

association scores, combined into one meta-program per perturbation, converted to SNP annotations 1759 

via variant-to-gene links and scored by stratified LD score regression, giving a trait relevance score 1760 

(TRS) per perturbation–trait pair. 1761
