Abstract —Predicting transcriptional responses to unseen ge-netic perturbations is essential for extending CRISPR screens across vast target–gene combinations. However, models can achieve strong aggregate predictive performance by reproducing historical gene-level response frequencies without adequately distinguishing the effects of different perturbations. Reliable prediction at the transcriptome scale is further challenged by uncertain response labels and redundant computation across millions of target–gene pairs. We present S1Pert, a System-One framework for target-specific gene response prediction with non-autoregressive online inference. S1Pert combines di-rectional Welch tests and equivalence testing to distinguish statistically supported responses from inconclusive observations. A frozen System-One encoder extracts reusable semantic rep-resentations of perturbation targets, query genes, and historical evidence. Compact pair-specific interactions are then computed and cached offline. A lightweight hierarchical cross-attention decoder integrates these representations with cross-target pair-wise ranking and synchronized historical-evidence dropout to reduce reliance on gene-level response frequencies. Extensive experiments demonstrate that S1Pert improves target-specific response discrimination while maintaining reliable prediction and efficient inference across diverse cellular contexts. These findings highlight the potential of System-One decision learning for scalable perturbation-response prediction beyond historical gene-level response tendencies. The source code is publicly available at https://github.com/liujiawen-jpg/S1Pert. 

Index Terms —Genetic Perturbation Prediction, Scientific Data Management, Data Quality and Curation, Heterogeneous Evi-dence Integration, System-One Decision Learning.

I. I NTRODUCTION 

High-throughput CRISPR screens have enabled systematic measurement of transcriptional responses to genetic perturba-tions [1], [2], yet the vast number of possible perturbation– gene combinations remains beyond experimental capacity [3]. Computational approaches seek to predict responses to unseen perturbation targets [4], either by estimating continuous ex-pression profiles from gene representations and biological net-works [3], [5]–[8] or by classifying response directions using biological knowledge and language-based reasoning [9]–[13]. Despite advances in predictive performance, strong results on aggregate metrics do not necessarily establish whether a model distinguishes the effects of different perturbations on the same gene or merely reproduces its historical response tendency. Genes differ substantially in how frequently they respond to genetic interventions [2], [14]. Some genes are repeatedly upregulated or downregulated across diverse perturbations, allowing models to achieve strong aggregate performance by learning historical gene-level response frequencies. Such predictions may be accurate on average while remaining largely insensitive to the identity of the perturbed target. Consistent with this, recent benchmarks have shown that simple baselines can remain competitive with more complex perturbation predictors [15], [16]. Even under target-held-out evaluation, a gene’s historical response tendency remains informative across unseen interventions, so aggregate metrics alone do not establish whether predictions depend on the perturbed target. Categorical perturbation-response datasets may classify statistically nonsignificant results as unchanged [9], although nonsignificance does not establish equivalence [17], [18]. As illustrated in Fig. 1, observa-tions with wide uncertainty intervals straddle the no-change equivalence zone and remain statistically inconclusive. Naive differential expression (DE) labeling merges these inconclu-sive responses into the unchanged class, introducing excess unchanged supervision [19]–[21] and distorting historical re-sponse priors, which biases the trained predictor toward out-putting no change for genuinely upregulated or downregulated test pairs. At scale, millions of target–gene pairs share gene annotations, perturbation descriptions, and historical evidence templates. Repeatedly encoding shared text or generating autoregressive rationales for individual pairs incurs substantial computational costs [10], [22]–[24]. Here we introduce S1Pert, a System-One perturbation-response predictor that combines statistically adjudicated su-pervision, reusable semantic representations, and single-pass, non-autoregressive decision-making. To improve label reli-ability, S1Pert integrates directional Welch tests with two-one-sided equivalence testing (TOST) [18], [25], retaining supported upregulation ( ↑), equivalence-supported unchanged responses ( =), and supported downregulation ( ↓) while ex-cluding statistically inconclusive observations ( ?) from training supervision (Fig. 1). S1Pert represents perturbation targets and readout genes through separate Condition and Query streams. A Historical Evidence stream summarizes out-of-fold response tendencies across training perturbations. To reduce redundant encoding, a frozen Jev model [26] processes |T | + |G| + 109 

unique full-length inputs for |T | perturbation targets, |G| query genes, and 109 historical-evidence templates. Pair-specific in-teractions are preserved through compact 36-token sequences processed by the encoder’s upper eight layers, with both evidence-present and evidence-masked representations cached offline. During online inference, a lightweight hierarchical cross-attention decoder estimates expression-change and con-ditional direction probabilities without autoregressive genera-tion. Cross-target pairwise ranking encourages the model to distinguish different perturbations acting on the same query gene, while synchronized historical-evidence dropout reduces reliance on historical response frequencies. Across four cellular benchmarks, S1Pert consistently out-performs state-of-the-art baselines in directional decision ac-curacy, within-gene target discrimination, and online inference efficiency. > •

A. Target-Specific Perturbation Prediction 

computational frameworks for unseen-target perturbation prediction broadly follow two paradigms [27], [28]. Contin-uous profile predictors such as GEARS [3] and STATE [29] regress post-perturbation transcriptomic states from gene co-expression graphs, biological ontologies, and context-aware state transitions [8]. Complementarily, discrete response clas-sifiers such as ALIGNED [13] integrate symbolic regulatory rules or multimodal priors [11] to categorize gene-level out-comes into upregulation, invariance, or downregulation. However, high aggregate accuracy under target-held-out evaluation does not guarantee genuine target-level discrimina-tion [16]. In data mining and recommender systems, models trained with pointwise supervision over skewed relational interactions are known to suffer from popularity and frequency bias, achieving low aggregate error by memorizing entity-level marginal frequencies rather than resolving pair-specific inter-actions [30], [31]. An analogous shortcut arises in perturbation screening, where predictors can minimize overall loss by reciting a query gene’s dominant historical response tendency across training interventions while remaining insensitive to the perturbed target. Extending counterfactual and deconfounded ranking principles from the data engineering literature [30], [31] to biological screen modeling, S1Pert decouples target, query, and prior streams and jointly optimizes cross-target pairwise ranking with synchronized evidence dropout to re-cover target-specific regulatory polarity. 

B. Statistical Data Cleaning and Materialized LLM Inference 

Downstream model reliability is fundamentally bounded by supervision quality, motivating extensive data engineering research on data cleaning for statistical modeling [32], proba-bilistic data repair [33], and learning under noisy labels [20]. In single-cell perturbation screens, standard differential expres-sion pipelines [17], [34], [35] default to an unchanged label whenever directional hypotheses fail to reach significance [11], [36], conflating low-depth sampling noise with biological invariance. To separate genuine invariance from insufficient statistical power, S1Pert adapts the two-one-sided tests (TOST) framework [25] into a pre-training data adjudication operator, combining scaled Welch statistics [37], [38] with equivalence bounds to filter inconclusive observations before supervision and prior construction. Integrating pretrained foundation models into perturba-tion screening introduces a second data engineering bottle-neck: scalable execution over millions of entity pairs. While scGPT [39] pretrains single-cell transformers, GenePT [40], [41] embeds functional text descriptions, and SUMMER [9] prompts language models with biological knowledge and em-pirical priors, applying large language models across Cartesian target–gene spaces incurs severe redundancy from repeatedly encoding shared context and decoding autoregressive ratio-nales [42]. Recent database and systems studies address bulk LLM analytics through declarative semantic operators [43], [44], flash-resident KV-cache management [45], and modular attention reuse across overlapping prompts [46]. Building on these view-materialization and state-reuse principles, S1Pert adopts a System-One decision paradigm [26] that factorizes pair-level inference into linear single-stream landmark pre-computation and compact upper-layer joint materialization, enabling fast, non-autoregressive hierarchical decoding over transcriptome-scale target–gene combinations. B. Overview 

Fig. 2 illustrates the overall framework of the proposed S1Pert, which consists of three key stages: Tri-Source Evi-dence Construction (Fig. 2-I), Multilevel Landmark Caching (Fig. 2-II), and Hierarchical Conditional Decoding (Fig. 2-III). Tri-Source Evidence Construction organizes functional annota-tions, control sequencing matrices, and target-level cross-fitted statistics into textual prompts (T Ci , T Qj , T Eij ) and continuous covariates (xCi , xQj , xEij ). Multilevel Landmark Caching ex-tracts compressed decision features offline via a parameter-shared frozen Jev backbone while projecting the continuous covariates into numerical features through trainable MLPs. Conditioned on both feature streams, Hierarchical Conditional Decoding uses a cross-attention decoder to predict a deci-sion vector (c, d ) that factorizes expression-change probability 

σ(c) and directional polarity σ(d) for unseen perturbation prediction. Finally, random evidence dropout, binary cross-entropy loss, and pairwise ranking loss jointly optimize the decoder to preserve target-specific separability and suppress gene-frequency shortcuts.

C. Tri-Source Evidence Construction 

Response prediction for an untested CRISPRi target i and a readout gene j depends on the target’s functional context, the query gene’s regulatory and basal-expression properties, and response patterns observed across prior perturbations. Specifically, the basal expression and functional context of target gene i shape the perturbation effect [2]. For query gene j, regulatory connections inform the response, while basal expression and variability influence its statistical de-tectability [1], [17]. Historical perturbation profiles further characterize gene-specific responsiveness and stress-response programs shared across interventions [2], [14]. To represent these complementary factors, S1Pert encodes each target–gene pair (i, j ) using condition ( C), query ( Q), and historical-evidence ( E) streams. Each stream integrates its structured prompt schema in Table I with the associated continuous covariates. All compared methods undergo task-specific adaptation for three-class response prediction. For continuous perturbation profile predictors, predicted expression changes are mapped to response classes using thresholds calibrated on the validation set. 

Evaluation Metrics. Classification performance is evaluated using Macro-F1, balanced accuracy (BA), and class-wise Pre-cision, Recall, and F1-score. All methods are evaluated on the same five random target-held-out splits for each cell line, with results reported as mean ± sample standard deviation. The best and second-best results are highlighted in bold and underlined, respectively. 

Implementation Details. S1Pert is implemented in PyTorch and trained on a single NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 GPU. We instantiate the Jev-style System-One encoder using the open-source DECIDER-4B model [26]. The backbone is frozen, and its representations are precomputed and cached. The task-specific decoder is trained from scratch using AdamW with a learning rate of 10 −4, weight decay of 10 −2, and a batch size of 4,096 perturbation–query gene pairs. Training runs for up to 100 epochs, with a minimum of 10 epochs and early stopping after 10 epochs without a validation Macro-F1 improvement exceeding 10 −4. The checkpoint with the highest validation Macro-F1 is selected for testing. Hyperparameters are fixed across cell lines and the five random target-held-out splits.

Offline landmark caching thus avoids repeated backbone computation during online inference and makes foundation-model perturbation screening practical at transcriptome scale. V. C ONCLUSION 

In this work, we propose S1Pert, a unified framework for directional perturbation decision prediction across unseen genetic targets. By integrating equivalence-based statistical ad-judication, split-cached foundation-model landmark encoding, and factorized cross-attentive readout, S1Pert addresses key challenges in filtering low-depth sampling noise, overcoming foundation-model inference bottlenecks, and preventing his-torical prior shortcuts. Experiments on four real-world single-cell perturbation benchmarks show that S1Pert outperforms existing baselines in directional decision accuracy, within-gene target ranking, and runtime efficiency. Designed for transcriptome-scale screening, S1Pert serves as a practical in silico prioritization tool that narrows down candidate genetic interventions before wet-lab validation. Future work will ana-lyze boundary failure cases, extend the framework to combina-torial multi-gene perturbations and multimodal cellular signals, and scale to broader tissue contexts while preserving statistical rigor and efficiency.
